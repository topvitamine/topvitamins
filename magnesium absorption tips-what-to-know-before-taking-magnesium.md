Quick answer summary

- Magnesium supports muscle and nerve function, bone health, energy metabolism, and electrolyte balance.
- Deficiency symptoms may include fatigue, cramps, anxiety, and irregular heartbeats.
- Absorption is improved with vitamin D and B6 and when taken with food; calcium and certain medications may reduce it.
- Excess supplemental magnesium can cause diarrhea and nausea—observe tolerable upper limits.
- Natural sources like nuts, seeds, and leafy greens are reliable ways to meet needs.

Introduction

Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in over 300 biochemical reactions, from ATP production to muscle contraction and nervous system regulation. Before starting a supplement, it helps to understand benefits, deficiency signs, absorption factors, safety considerations, and dietary sources. Key benefits

Magnesium contributes to:

- Muscle relaxation and prevention of cramps, acting with calcium to regulate contraction and release.
- Nervous system function through neurotransmitter balance that can influence mood and sleep.
- Energy metabolism as a cofactor for enzymes in ATP synthesis.
- Bone health by participating in mineralization and calcium regulation.
- Cardiovascular stability, supporting normal heart rhythm and blood pressure regulation.

Recognizing deficiency and risk groups

Magnesium deficiency (hypomagnesemia) is often subtle because only about 1% of body magnesium circulates in blood. Common signs include fatigue, muscle twitches, cramps, anxiety, palpitations, and sleep disturbances. Groups at higher risk include athletes (increased sweat loss), older adults (reduced absorption), pregnant women (higher demand), people with gastrointestinal disorders, those with type 2 diabetes, and individuals with heavy alcohol use.

Improving absorption and bioavailability

Magnesium absorption occurs mainly in the small intestine and is influenced by other nutrients, supplement form, and gut health. Tips to maximize uptake:

- Take magnesium with food to improve absorption and reduce digestive upset.
- Combine with vitamin D and B6, which support intestinal absorption and intracellular use.
- Select bioavailable forms such as magnesium citrate, glycinate, or malate rather than poorly absorbed oxides.
- Avoid taking high doses of calcium or certain medications at the same time; some drugs (e.g., proton pump inhibitors, certain antibiotics) can impair absorption.

Safety, dosing, and practical guidance

The EFSA tolerable upper intake level for supplemental magnesium in healthy adults is 350 mg/day (this excludes magnesium naturally obtained from food). Excess supplemental intake commonly causes diarrhea, nausea, and abdominal cramping. People with kidney impairment should be cautious because reduced excretion can lead to dangerously high serum magnesium. Magnesium can interact with antibiotics, bisphosphonates, diuretics, and some cardiac medications—check with a healthcare professional when on long-term prescriptions.

Dietary sources and practical meals

Prefer food first: dark leafy greens, nuts and seeds, whole grains, legumes, fatty fish, avocados, bananas, and high-cocoa dark chocolate are rich sources.
