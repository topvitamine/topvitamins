## Introduction: how omega-3 works in the body

Omega-3 fatty acids influence many physiological systems when introduced through diet or supplements. The three primary forms—ALA, EPA and DHA—are used differently by the body: ALA (plant-derived) is a precursor, while EPA and DHA (marine-derived) are incorporated directly into tissues. For a concise overview of systemic effects, see [what happens to the body when you take omega-3?](https://www.topvitamine.com/blogs/news/what-happens-to-body-when-you-take-omega-3).

## Absorption and cellular integration

Once consumed, omega-3s are absorbed in the small intestine and transported in chylomicrons through the lymphatic system. The chemical form (re-esterified triglyceride vs. ethyl ester) affects bioavailability. EPA and DHA are readily integrated into cell membranes, altering membrane fluidity, receptor function, and intracellular signaling—changes most evident in heart, brain and retinal tissues.

## Cardiovascular effects

EPA and DHA reduce triglyceride synthesis in the liver and can lower circulating triglyceride levels substantially at therapeutic doses. They support endothelial function and vasodilation, which may modestly reduce blood pressure, and they appear to stabilize cardiac electrical activity, decreasing arrhythmia risk in some populations. Large clinical trials and meta-analyses provide evidence for reduced cardiovascular events in selected groups, though individual benefit depends on dose and baseline risk.

## Inflammation modulation

At the molecular level, EPA and DHA displace arachidonic acid in membranes and are metabolized into bioactive mediators (resolvins, protectins) that actively resolve inflammation. Supplementation is associated with reductions in biomarkers such as CRP and certain pro-inflammatory cytokines, and clinical improvements have been observed in conditions like rheumatoid arthritis and some inflammatory skin disorders.

## Brain and mental health

DHA is a major structural lipid in neuronal membranes and supports synaptic function and neuroplasticity. Observational studies and randomized trials suggest benefits for cognition, memory, and mood—particularly when EPA-rich formulas are used for depressive symptoms. Adequate DHA during pregnancy and early life is important for neural development.

## Lipid profile and metabolic effects

Omega-3s primarily reduce triglycerides by lowering hepatic VLDL output and enhancing triglyceride clearance. Effects on LDL and HDL are variable; some preparations slightly increase LDL particle concentration while shifting particle size to less atherogenic forms. They also improve insulin sensitivity in some studies, contributing to metabolic benefits.

## Hormonal and endocrine influences

Omega-3s can modulate endocrine signaling by influencing adipokines (e.g., adiponectin) and the HPA axis, which may reduce cortisol responses to stress. These effects support insulin action and can benefit conditions such as PCOS by improving hormone ratios and inflammatory status.

## Summary

Omega-3 fatty acids act on membranes, inflammatory pathways, lipid metabolism, brain structure and endocrine signaling. Their measurable effects depend on dose, form and individual health status. When used appropriately and under professional guidance, they are a well-supported component of nutritional strategies aimed at cardiovascular, cognitive and inflammatory health.
