## Introduction

Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in energy production, muscle contraction, nerve transmission and bone health. While dietary magnesium is generally safe, supplemental magnesium can cause side effects in some people — especially at higher doses, with specific chemical forms, or when kidney function is impaired. ## Common side effects and why they occur

The most frequent adverse reactions to oral magnesium are gastrointestinal. Magnesium has an osmotic laxative effect: unabsorbed magnesium in the gut draws water into the bowel, which can cause loose stools, cramping and nausea. This is most pronounced with salts that have lower bioavailability, such as magnesium oxide and magnesium citrate. By contrast, chelated forms (for example, magnesium glycinate or malate) tend to be better tolerated.

Dose matters: many mild side effects are dose-dependent and often resolve when the dose is reduced. Individual factors — existing gastrointestinal disease, sensitivity to laxatives, or concurrent medications — influence susceptibility to GI adverse effects.

## Signs of toxicity (hypermagnesemia)

True magnesium toxicity is uncommon in people with normal renal function, because the kidneys efficiently excrete excess magnesium. However, excessive supplemental intake or impaired renal clearance can produce hypermagnesemia, with symptoms that may progress from drowsiness and hypotension to bradycardia, respiratory depression and in severe cases coma. Neuromuscular signs include weakness and diminished reflexes. If toxicity is suspected, prompt medical evaluation is required.

## Who should be cautious

People with chronic kidney disease are at highest risk because reduced glomerular filtration impairs magnesium excretion. Older adults may also be more vulnerable due to age-related renal decline and polypharmacy. Some medications interact with magnesium: certain diuretics, lithium, and some antibiotics (e.g., tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones) can lead to altered absorption or clearance. Pregnancy merits individualized assessment to balance maternal needs and fetal safety.

## Magnesium oxide: specific considerations

Magnesium oxide is widely available and contains a high proportion of elemental magnesium, but its low absorption rate means much remains in the gut, increasing the likelihood of diarrhea and discomfort. Because of its poor bioavailability, some people may increase the dose to compensate, which can raise the risk of accumulation, particularly in those with reduced renal function.

## When supplementation may be indicated

Clinical signs that can suggest low magnesium include muscle cramps or spasms, unexplained fatigue, palpitations, and sleep disturbances. Laboratory testing (serum magnesium, and when needed, intracellular measures) helps confirm deficiency. ## Summary

Magnesium supplements can provide important benefits but are not free from side effects. Gastrointestinal issues are most common; toxicity is rare but possible in the context of high supplemental doses or kidney impairment.
