# Which drink is rich in vitamin D?

Vitamin D is essential for calcium absorption, bone health, immune function, and mood regulation. Sunlight exposure remains a primary source, but dietary intake from foods and beverages can be important—especially in regions with limited ultraviolet B (UVB) exposure. This article summarizes the most relevant drink-based sources of vitamin D, comparing fortified beverages, natural liquid sources, and concentrated supplements.

For an in-depth overview, see [Which drink is rich in vitamin D?](https://www.topvitamine.com/blogs/news/which-drink-is-rich-in-vitamin-d).

Fortified beverages are among the most accessible sources. Many dairy and plant-based milks, as well as some orange juices, are fortified with vitamin D (commonly 100–150 IU per cup). Fortification typically uses either vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) or D2 (ergocalciferol); D3 is generally more effective at raising and maintaining serum 25(OH)D levels. When choosing fortified drinks, review labels for the form and amount of vitamin D and any added sugars.

Milk options available in most markets include:

- Fortified cow’s milk (often around 120 IU per cup in fortified systems).
- Fortified soy, almond, oat, and other plant-based milks (commonly 100–150 IU per cup).

Natural liquid sources are less common but can provide substantial amounts. Emulsified cod liver oil drinks and concentrated fish-oil formulations can contain several hundred to over a thousand IU per serving, while mushroom-based beverages made from UV-exposed fungi deliver vitamin D2 in smaller amounts. These natural liquids may also supply complementary nutrients such as EPA, DHA, or vitamin A, but consumers should note dietary preferences and potential allergens.

For targeted dosing, liquid vitamin D supplements (drops, liposomal solutions, or pre-mixed liquids) offer precise control and are useful for infants, older adults, and individuals with malabsorption. Health authorities often recommend around 600 IU/day for most adults, with an upper tolerable limit near 4000 IU/day; individualized needs should be determined with medical guidance and periodic blood testing.

Plant-based vitamin D drinks are increasingly available for vegans and those with dairy intolerance. Advances in sourcing now allow D3 from lichen (vegan D3), and fortified plant milks can be a reliable source when labeled appropriately. Pairing vitamin D intake with magnesium and dietary fats enhances absorption, since vitamin D is fat-soluble.
