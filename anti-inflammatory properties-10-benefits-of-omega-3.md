## Introduction

Omega‑3 fatty acids are essential polyunsaturated fats with well-documented roles in cellular structure, inflammation control, and brain function. Because the body cannot synthesize them de novo, they must be obtained from diet or supplements. A growing body of clinical and observational research supports multiple health benefits of EPA and DHA (marine omega‑3s) and ALA (plant-based omega‑3), which are summarized below.

## Top 10 evidence-based benefits of Omega‑3

1. Heart health — EPA and DHA help lower triglycerides, may modestly reduce blood pressure, and support vascular function, which collectively reduce cardiovascular risk markers.

2. Anti-inflammatory effects — Omega‑3s are precursors to resolvins and protectins that actively resolve inflammation and can reduce circulating inflammatory cytokines in chronic inflammatory states.

3. Cognitive support — DHA is a major structural lipid in the brain; higher omega‑3 status is associated with better cognitive development in children and slower age‑related decline in older adults.

4. Mental health and mood regulation — Clinical trials suggest omega‑3s, particularly EPA-rich formulations, can be an adjunctive strategy for depressive symptoms and mood stabilization in some populations.

5. Joint health — Regular omega‑3 intake can reduce joint pain and stiffness in inflammatory arthropathies and may reduce reliance on nonsteroidal anti‑inflammatory medications for some patients.

6. Visual development and eye health — DHA is concentrated in the retina; adequate intake supports visual development in infants and may help maintain retinal function with aging.

7. Metabolic benefits — Omega‑3s can improve triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and may support insulin sensitivity and a healthier lipid profile when combined with diet and exercise.

8. Pregnancy and neonatal outcomes — Maternal DHA intake supports fetal brain and eye development and is linked to beneficial neonatal outcomes when consumed at recommended levels.

9. Immune modulation — Omega‑3s modulate immune cell activity and inflammatory signaling, contributing to balanced immune responses rather than broad immunosuppression.

10. Cellular membrane and metabolic function — By influencing membrane fluidity and receptor function, omega‑3s affect cellular signaling, nutrient transport, and mitochondrial efficiency.
