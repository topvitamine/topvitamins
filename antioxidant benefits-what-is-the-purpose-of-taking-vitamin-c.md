## Quick overview

Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is an essential, water-soluble nutrient with well-documented roles in immune function, antioxidant defense, and collagen synthesis. Because humans cannot synthesize it, dietary intake or supplementation is required to maintain physiological levels that support tissue repair, protect against oxidative damage, and contribute to overall resilience during periods of stress or illness.

## Key physiological roles

- Immune support: Vitamin C supports innate and adaptive immunity by enhancing white blood cell function, improving barrier integrity of the skin, and helping to modulate inflammatory responses.
- Antioxidant protection: It neutralizes reactive oxygen species (ROS), helping to limit cellular damage from environmental stressors and metabolism.
- Collagen production: As a required cofactor for enzymes that hydroxylate proline and lysine, vitamin C is essential for stable collagen formation, which underpins skin, bone, and connective tissue health.
- Wound healing and tissue maintenance: Adequate vitamin C accelerates epithelial repair and supports vascular integrity.

## Evidence on colds and infections

Randomized trials and systematic reviews indicate that regular vitamin C supplementation does not consistently prevent the common cold in the general population, but it can reduce the duration and severity of symptoms, particularly in people exposed to acute physical stress (e.g., athletes) or with suboptimal dietary intake. Short-term increases in dose at the onset of illness have mixed evidence; preventive, daily supplementation shows more consistent modest benefits.

## Practical considerations for supplementation

Daily intake recommendations vary by authority; many regions suggest an intake around 80 mg/day for adults as a baseline. Therapeutic supplemental amounts commonly range from 500–1000 mg/day in practice, while tolerable upper limits (commonly set near 2000 mg/day) reflect gastrointestinal tolerability rather than systemic toxicity because excess vitamin C is water-soluble and is excreted in urine.

Formulations include tablets, powders, buffered or “slow-release” forms, liposomal preparations that aim to increase cellular uptake, and topical products for dermatologic use. ## Summary

Vitamin C plays multiple, evidence-supported roles in human physiology—most notably antioxidant defense, immune function, and collagen synthesis. Supplementation can help maintain adequate levels when dietary intake is insufficient and may modestly reduce the duration and severity of respiratory infections. Dose selection should account for dietary intake, health status, and tolerability, and clinicians or pharmacists can provide personalized advice based on individual needs.
