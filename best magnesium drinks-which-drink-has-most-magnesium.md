## Which drink has the most magnesium?

Magnesium is an essential mineral that supports energy production, nerve and muscle function, bone health, and cardiovascular regulation. Dietary shortfalls are common, so beverages—both natural and fortified—can be useful contributors to total intake. This article summarizes which drinks tend to provide the most magnesium, how bioavailability varies, and practical considerations when choosing a beverage-based strategy.

Natural sources in liquid form

Mineral waters sourced from magnesium-rich aquifers are among the densest naturally occurring liquid sources of magnesium. Bottled mineral waters can range from roughly 20 mg/L to more than 100 mg/L depending on geology. Vegetable juices and green smoothies that include spinach, chard, kale, avocado, or seeds (pumpkin, chia) can also supply meaningful amounts; concentrated blends can deliver tens to over a hundred milligrams per serving. Coconut water provides modest magnesium alongside potassium and electrolytes.

Fortified and supplement beverages

The highest magnesium concentrations in beverages are typically found in fortified or supplement products formulated for therapeutic or replacement use. Powdered mixes and ready-to-drink supplements using bioavailable forms such as magnesium citrate or magnesium bisglycinate commonly provide 200–400 mg of elemental magnesium per serving. These products are designed to deliver targeted doses and are usually labeled with the magnesium form and amount.

Bioavailability and form

Not all magnesium compounds are absorbed equally. Magnesium citrate and bisglycinate are generally better absorbed than magnesium oxide or carbonate. For people sensitive to gastrointestinal effects, bisglycinate is often better tolerated. When assessing a drink, check the label for the chemical form of magnesium as well as the elemental magnesium amount.

Practical comparison

- Highest per-serving concentrations: supplement drinks and magnesium-fortified powders (200–400 mg/serving).
- High natural liquid sources: concentrated green smoothies or certain mineral waters (up to ~100+ mg per liter for some brands).
- Moderate natural sources: coconut water, seed-based smoothies, and vegetable juices (tens of mg per serving).

Timing and integration

Consider when and how you consume magnesium drinks: many people take supplement beverages in the evening to support relaxation and muscle recovery, while athletes may prefer electrolyte blends post-exercise. Consistency and dosing matter more than occasional large intakes; daily routines that combine natural magnesium-rich beverages with, if needed, a targeted supplement drink can be effective.

Safety and labeling

Review product labels for elemental magnesium per serving, the specific magnesium compound used, caloric and sugar content, and any added ingredients. High-dose magnesium supplements can cause gastrointestinal symptoms in some individuals and may interact with certain medications; consult a healthcare professional before beginning regular high-dose use.
