Magnesium supports more than 300 enzymatic reactions in the body, including energy production, nerve transmission, muscle contraction, and bone health. Many adults fall short of recommended intakes (about 310–420 mg/day depending on age and sex), and beverages can be a practical way to improve magnesium status. This article reviews evidence-based drink options that deliver meaningful amounts of magnesium and how to choose between natural sources and formulated products.

Natural mineral waters are among the simplest liquid sources of magnesium. Depending on geology, bottled mineral waters can contain anywhere from 20 mg/L to over 100 mg/L of magnesium. Higher-magnesium brands can contribute a significant portion of daily needs simply through regular hydration. For precise amounts, check the label for “Mg2+” reported in mg/L.

Coconut water is a readily available natural beverage with electrolytes and modest magnesium content (roughly 60 mg per liter, variable by brand). It provides a rehydrating, low-calorie option after exercise and supplies potassium alongside magnesium, which supports cellular electrolyte balance.

Plant-based drinks and smoothies allow concentrated magnesium delivery from whole-food ingredients. Leafy greens (spinach, Swiss chard) blended into juices or smoothies provide bioavailable magnesium along with fiber, vitamin K, and phytonutrients. Seeds and nuts—especially pumpkin seeds, almonds, and chia—are dense sources of magnesium and can be added to smoothies for both texture and mineral boost. Including a source of healthy fat (e.g., avocado or nut butter) improves absorption of fat-soluble cofactors and overall nutrient uptake.

Cacao or raw chocolate beverages are notable for high magnesium content: cacao powder contains substantial magnesium per 100 g, and a cup of cocoa can add a meaningful amount to intake. Unsweetened preparations keep added sugars low while delivering polyphenols alongside minerals.

Herbal teas made from mineral-rich plants (e.g., nettle leaf) contain small but useful amounts of magnesium and can be a gentle daily source when brewed consistently. Preparing teas with mineral water can incrementally increase magnesium intake over time.

For predictable dosing, magnesium supplement beverages—powdered mixes or ready-to-drink formulations—offer standardized amounts and well-absorbed forms (such as citrate, malate, or glycinate). Liquid formats are often tolerated better than large oral tablets and can be timed for post-exercise recovery or for alleviating occasional cramps. When choosing a product, review the form of magnesium, the elemental magnesium per serving, and any added ingredients that may affect tolerance.

Practical tips:

- Read labels: bottled waters and commercial drinks should list magnesium content in mg/L or mg per serving.
- Balance sources: combining natural drinks (mineral water, coconut water, smoothies) with whole-food meals helps avoid excessive intake while improving overall nutrient quality.
- Consider absorption: some forms and co-nutrients (vitamin B6, adequate energy intake) support magnesium uptake.
- Monitor symptoms and medication interactions: certain diuretics or gastrointestinal conditions affect magnesium needs and losses; consult a clinician if you suspect deficiency.
