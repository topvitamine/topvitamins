**Introduction**

Daily nutrition forms the backbone of health, but modern diets and lifestyle factors can leave gaps that affect energy, immunity, and cognitive performance. Supplements can help fill those gaps when chosen based on evidence, individual needs, and safety guidelines such as those from the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA).

[Which dietary supplements should you take every day?](https://www.topvitamine.com/blogs/news/which-dietary-supplements-should-you-take-every-day)

**Core daily supplements to consider**

- Multivitamin: A well-formulated multivitamin provides a baseline of essential micronutrients (B vitamins, vitamins A, C, D, E, K, and trace minerals). It is useful when dietary variety is limited or to ensure consistent intake of RDAs.

- Vitamin D: Deficiency is common in regions with limited sun exposure or during winter. Daily vitamin D supports bone health, immune function, and muscle performance; dosing should reflect baseline status and seasonal variation.

- Magnesium: Involved in hundreds of enzymatic reactions, magnesium supports sleep, energy metabolism, and neuromuscular function. Forms like magnesium glycinate or citrate offer good bioavailability.

- Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA): Long-chain omega-3s support cardiovascular and brain health. Regular intake via fatty fish or supplements is often recommended, especially for individuals with low dietary fish consumption.

**Targeted minerals and nutrients**

Zinc and selenium play roles in immune defence and antioxidant pathways; iron is essential for oxygen transport but requires monitoring because excess iron can be harmful. Choice and timing matter: some minerals compete for absorption, so separating doses or using balanced formulations can improve uptake.

**Specialized daily supports and boosters**

Probiotics may support gut health and mucosal immunity for some individuals. B12 is critical for those on vegetarian or vegan diets. Adaptogens (e.g., ashwagandha) and specific amino acids can be useful as nutritional boosters for stress resilience and recovery, but evidence varies and long-term safety data differ by compound.

**Practical guidance and safety**

1. Personalize based on diet, age, sex, life stage, and clinical tests. Bloodwork provides objective data for nutrients like vitamin D, B12, and iron.
2. Prioritize food first; supplements complement a nutrient-dense diet and cannot replace fibre, phytochemicals, or food synergy.
3. Follow recommended intakes and be mindful of fat-soluble vitamin accumulation (A, D, E, K) and interactions with medications.
4. Choose products with transparent ingredient lists and third-party testing when possible.
