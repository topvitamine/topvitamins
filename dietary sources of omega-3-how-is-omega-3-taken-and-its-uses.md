Understanding omega-3 fatty acids and how to take them helps people make informed choices about diet and supplements. Omega-3s — primarily EPA, DHA, and ALA — support cell membranes, cardiovascular function, brain development, and inflammatory balance. Because the body cannot synthesize these fats efficiently, they must be consumed through food or supplements.

Dietary sources remain the preferred option for many. Cold-water oily fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines) provide substantial EPA and DHA with high bioavailability. For vegetarians and vegans, plant foods such as flaxseed, chia, hemp and walnuts supply ALA, which the body converts to EPA/DHA only inefficiently. Algal oil supplements deliver preformed DHA (and sometimes EPA) and are a practical plant-based alternative.

Deciding how to take omega-3 depends on goals and circumstances. General evidence-based dosage guidance suggests 250–500 mg combined EPA and DHA daily for healthy adults to support normal heart function, consistent with EFSA recommendations. Pregnant and breastfeeding women are often advised to ensure additional DHA intake for fetal and infant neural development. Higher therapeutic doses (up to several grams daily) may be used under clinician supervision to lower very high triglycerides or to address specific inflammatory conditions; the tolerable supplemental intake for most people is generally considered up to 5 g/day, but many clinicians prefer staying within 2–3 g/day unless monitored.

Supplement form and timing influence effectiveness and tolerability. Fish oil is the most researched supplement source and typically comes as triglyceride or ethyl ester forms; triglyceride forms may offer somewhat better absorption. Algal oils provide a vegan option. Taking omega-3s with a meal that contains fat improves absorption and can reduce gastrointestinal side effects, such as reflux or fishy aftertaste. Store oils in a cool, dark place and look for products that use molecular distillation or third-party testing to limit contaminants.

When evaluating a supplement, check the label for EPA and DHA content per serving rather than total fish oil volume. Purity certifications and sustainability labels can also guide choice.
