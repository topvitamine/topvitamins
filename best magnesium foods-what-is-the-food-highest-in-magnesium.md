Introduction

Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in hundreds of enzymatic reactions, supporting muscle and nerve function, energy production, and bone health. Modern diets and soil depletion mean many people fall short of recommended intakes, so identifying magnesium-dense foods helps create a practical, food-first strategy for meeting needs.

Which food is highest in magnesium?

Although several foods provide substantial magnesium, pumpkin seeds (pepitas) stand out for concentration by weight. One ounce (about 28 g) of pumpkin seeds supplies roughly 150 mg of magnesium, making them one of the richest single-food sources. Other top contributors include chia seeds, almonds, quinoa, black beans, spinach, avocados, and dark chocolate. Comparing common magnesium-rich foods

- Pumpkin seeds: Extremely high per ounce; also deliver zinc, iron, and healthy fats. Their compact density makes them an efficient way to boost intake.
- Chia seeds: About 95 mg per 28 g serving, plus fiber and omega-3 ALA; easily added to yogurts and smoothies.
- Spinach: Raw spinach contains ~79 mg per 100 g; one cup cooked concentrates magnesium (~157 mg), along with iron, folate, and vitamin K.
- Black beans and quinoa: Legumes and pseudo-cereals provide roughly 100–120 mg per cooked cup, with protein and fiber that support overall nutrient absorption.
- Almonds and dark chocolate: Nuts and high-cocoa chocolate are convenient snack sources that contribute meaningful magnesium along with antioxidants and healthy fats.

Dietary context and absorption

Whole foods provide magnesium alongside fiber, protein, healthy fats, and complementary minerals, which can improve bioavailability compared with isolated minerals in some contexts. For example, pairing leafy greens with a source of healthy fat (olive oil, avocado) supports the absorption of fat-soluble nutrients and may influence overall meal nutrient uptake. Soaking or lightly cooking certain seeds and nuts can reduce phytic acid and modestly enhance mineral availability.

When diet may not be enough

Many people can meet magnesium needs through a varied diet. However, certain life stages, medications, gastrointestinal conditions, and chronic diseases can increase requirements or impair absorption. In those situations, a supplement may be appropriate after consultation with a healthcare professional. Practical tips

- Add a handful of pumpkin or chia seeds to salads, oatmeal, or smoothies daily.
- Use cooked spinach or quinoa as a side or base to combine magnesium with protein and other minerals.
- Choose high-cocoa dark chocolate (70%+) in small portions as an occasional nutrient-dense treat.

Conclusion

Pumpkin seeds are the single food highest in magnesium by weight, but a balanced approach that combines seeds, leafy greens, legumes, nuts, and whole grains ensures broader nutrient benefits and improved absorption. Where dietary intake falls short or needs are elevated, professional guidance can determine if supplementation is warranted.
