## Introduction

Omega-3 fatty acids, particularly EPA and DHA, are recognized for systemic benefits such as cardiovascular and cognitive support, but they also influence intestinal health. The intestines perform nutrient absorption, immunity and host a dynamic microbiome. Emerging evidence indicates omega-3s can affect gut barrier integrity, inflammatory signaling, microbiome composition, and nutrient uptake—mechanisms directly relevant to digestive function and resilience.

## Barrier integrity and epithelial function

Omega-3s are incorporated into enterocyte membranes, increasing membrane fluidity and supporting tight-junction function. This structural role can reduce epithelial permeability, a key factor in preventing translocation of bacterial components like lipopolysaccharide (LPS) into circulation. Reduced permeability helps limit systemic immune activation associated with so-called 'leaky gut'. Controlled trials and preclinical models report improvements in mucosal markers after EPA/DHA supplementation, particularly where inflammation or stress has compromised the lining.

## Modulation of intestinal inflammation

EPA and DHA compete with arachidonic acid for enzymatic pathways, shifting the profile of lipid mediators away from pro-inflammatory prostaglandins and leukotrienes toward specialized pro-resolving mediators (resolvins, protectins, maresins). These mediators help resolve inflammation and promote tissue repair. Clinical research in inflammatory bowel conditions and smaller trials in functional disorders suggest omega-3s can reduce disease activity or inflammatory biomarkers as an adjunct to standard care, though they are not a replacement for established therapies.

## Effects on the microbiome

Dietary omega-3s influence microbial composition and diversity. Observational and intervention studies associate higher omega-3 status with increased abundance of beneficial genera such as Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium and with greater overall microbial diversity—features linked to improved metabolic and immune outcomes. Animal studies also show omega-3s can support microbiome recovery after antibiotic exposure, suggesting a role in ecological resilience.

## Digestive function and nutrient absorption

Omega-3s contribute to digestive efficiency by supporting bile metabolism and enterocyte enzyme activity, which aid lipid digestion and the uptake of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K). By reducing chronic intestinal inflammation, they may help preserve villus structure and absorptive surface area, with potential downstream benefits for mineral and micronutrient status in conditions that impair absorption.

## Summary

Omega-3 fatty acids act on multiple intestinal mechanisms: they support epithelial integrity, modulate inflammatory pathways, shape the microbiome, and assist nutrient absorption. Evidence supports their role as a component of dietary strategies to maintain gut health, though clinical decisions—especially for inflammatory disorders—should integrate individual needs and medical guidance.
