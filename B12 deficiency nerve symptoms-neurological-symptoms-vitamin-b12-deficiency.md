## Introduction

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is essential for nervous system function, myelin synthesis, and DNA production. Deficiency may present subtly and progress to significant neurological impairment if not identified and managed early. This article summarizes common neurological signs, underlying mechanisms, diagnostic clues, and the role of nutritional support in prevention and recovery.

## How low B12 affects the nervous system

B12 participates in methylation reactions needed for myelin maintenance and neurotransmitter synthesis. Insufficient B12 disrupts these processes, causing demyelination and impaired neuronal signaling. Early effects typically involve peripheral nerves; with prolonged deficiency, central structures such as the dorsal columns of the spinal cord may be affected, producing broader sensory and motor deficits.

## Common neurological symptoms

- Peripheral neuropathy: tingling, numbness, “pins and needles,” or burning sensations in hands and feet. These are often the first signs and may precede obvious blood-test abnormalities.
- Gait disturbance and balance problems: impaired proprioception and coordination, especially noticeable when walking in low light or on uneven surfaces.
- Muscle weakness and reduced fine motor control: difficulty with stairs, buttoning, or handwriting in progressive cases.
- Cognitive changes: memory lapses, slowed thinking, difficulty concentrating, and 'mental fog.' In older adults this may resemble early dementia.
- Mood and psychiatric changes: depression, irritability, and in severe cases, psychosis.
- Visual disturbances: optic nerve involvement can cause blurred vision or visual field changes in advanced deficiency.

Recognizing these symptoms early increases the chance of recovery.

## Diagnosis and clinical evaluation

Laboratory testing commonly includes serum B12, methylmalonic acid (MMA), and homocysteine; MMA and homocysteine often rise before serum B12 falls, aiding early detection. A focused neurological exam assesses vibration sense, proprioception, reflexes, gait, and coordination. Imaging and electrophysiology may be used to rule out other causes when presentations are atypical.

## Management and nutritional considerations

Restoring adequate B12 levels halts progression and can reverse many early neurological symptoms. Treatment approaches depend on cause and severity: oral or sublingual supplementation may suffice for dietary deficiency, while intramuscular injections are preferred when absorption is impaired. Co-nutrients such as omega-3 fatty acids and magnesium support neuronal membrane health and function, and addressing overall nutritional status is important for recovery.

## Practical takeaways

If you notice unexplained numbness, balance changes, cognitive slowing, or new mood symptoms—especially if you are older, follow a plant-based diet, or have gastrointestinal disorders—consider medical evaluation and laboratory testing. Early detection and appropriate nutritional management minimize the risk of long-term nerve damage and improve functional outcomes.
