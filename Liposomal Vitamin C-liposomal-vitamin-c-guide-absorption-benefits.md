## Introduction

Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is essential for immune function, collagen synthesis, antioxidant defense, and tissue repair. However, the clinical and practical benefits of oral vitamin C depend on how much of the nutrient is actually absorbed and delivered to tissues. Liposomal delivery is an advanced formulation approach designed to improve bioavailability by encapsulating vitamin C in phospholipid vesicles that mimic cell membranes.

## How liposomal vitamin C works

Liposomal vitamin C encapsulates ascorbic acid within small phospholipid spheres (liposomes). These vesicles protect the payload from gastric degradation and can fuse with cell membranes or be taken up by endocytosis in the gut, allowing more vitamin C to reach systemic circulation than conventional tablets or powders. The underlying rationale is that liposomes bypass or reduce dependence on sodium-dependent vitamin C transporters (SVCTs), which saturate at higher doses.

Evidence from pharmacokinetic studies indicates that liposomal formulations can produce higher plasma concentrations of vitamin C compared with traditional oral forms, particularly at equivalent or lower doses. While study sizes and methodologies vary, the mechanism—protection from degradation and improved cellular uptake—is consistent across research reports.

## Benefits associated with improved absorption

Higher bioavailability can enhance vitamin C’s well-established physiological roles: supporting immune cell function, promoting collagen formation for skin and connective tissues, and providing antioxidant protection against oxidative stress. Improved tissue delivery may also support endothelial health and contribute to reduced arterial stiffness in some contexts. Some preliminary research suggests anti-inflammatory effects, but additional clinical trials are required to define the magnitude and relevance of these effects across populations.

## Considerations for formulation and selection

Effective liposomal products rely on high-quality phospholipids (commonly sunflower or soy lecithin), controlled particle size (often below 200 nm), and validated manufacturing methods such as sonication or extrusion to produce uniform liposomes. Liquid liposomal formulations are often studied for their absorption advantages, though encapsulated powders and capsules are also available. Transparency about ingredient sourcing, minimal use of unnecessary additives, and third-party testing improve the ability to evaluate a product’s quality.
