# What is the purpose of taking omega-3?

Omega-3 fatty acids are essential nutrients with well-documented roles in cardiovascular, neurological, immune and musculoskeletal health. Modern diets often supply inadequate amounts of long-chain omega-3s (EPA and DHA), which has prompted interest in dietary supplements to restore balance and support physiological function.

## Why people take omega-3

Omega-3s contribute to several core processes. EPA and DHA are integral to cell membrane structure, serve as precursors to signaling molecules that regulate inflammation and clotting, and influence gene expression related to lipid metabolism. ALA, a plant-derived omega-3, can be converted to EPA and DHA but the conversion efficiency is low in humans, so direct intake of EPA/DHA from marine or algal sources is more effective for many objectives.

Regular intake of EPA and DHA is associated with lower triglyceride concentrations, modest reductions in blood pressure, improved endothelial function and a decrease in arrhythmic risk. These mechanisms underlie omega-3’s role in cardiovascular protection. ## Inflammation and joint health

EPA modifies inflammatory signaling by competing with arachidonic acid and promoting the production of less pro-inflammatory eicosanoids, while also supporting specialized pro-resolving mediators (resolvins and protectins) that help terminate inflammation. Clinical studies have reported reductions in joint pain and morning stiffness in inflammatory arthritis with consistent omega-3 supplementation, and some patients reduce reliance on NSAIDs when omega-3s are part of management. Therapeutic effects for inflammatory conditions often require higher daily intakes of EPA+DHA under clinical supervision.

## Brain function and mood

DHA is highly concentrated in neuronal membranes and the retina; adequate DHA is important during fetal brain development and continues to support cognitive function across the lifespan. Research links higher DHA levels with better memory performance, and EPA has shown benefit in some trials for mood disorders when used as an adjunct to standard treatments. While omega-3s are not a standalone cure for neuropsychiatric conditions, they are a low-risk adjunct supported by mechanistic and clinical data.

## Practical points and safety

For general maintenance, many health organizations suggest roughly 250–500 mg/day of combined EPA and DHA, while therapeutic uses (e.g., triglyceride lowering or anti-inflammatory effects) often require higher doses under professional supervision. Omega-3 supplements are best absorbed when taken with dietary fat and are generally safe, though individuals on anticoagulant therapy or with specific medical conditions should consult a clinician.

## Bottom line

Omega-3s support heart, brain and joint health through lipid modulation, anti-inflammatory actions and structural roles in cell membranes. Where dietary intake of EPA and DHA is low, targeted supplementation can help achieve clinically meaningful effects when used appropriately and under guidance for higher-dose therapeutic needs.
