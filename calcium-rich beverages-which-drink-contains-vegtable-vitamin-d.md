Introduction

Vitamin D is essential for calcium absorption, bone health, immune function, and muscle performance. Sunlight is the primary natural source, but dietary intake becomes important for people with limited sun exposure, older adults, and those living at high latitudes. Drinks can be a practical way to increase vitamin D intake because many beverages are fortified or can incorporate vitamin D–rich ingredients.

Common vitamin D drinks

- Fortified cow’s milk and plant-based milks (soy, almond, oat) are among the most reliable beverage sources of vitamin D when fortified by manufacturers. These products often provide 2.5–5 µg (100–200 IU) per serving depending on the brand and country regulations.
- Fortified orange juice is another frequent choice for households seeking a non-dairy option that supplies both vitamin D and calcium.

For a focused overview of beverage options and fortification, see [Which drink contains vegetable vitamin D](https://www.topvitamine.com/blogs/news/which-drink-contains-vegtable-vitamin-d).

Types and nutritional considerations

Fortified dairy drinks: In many countries, milk is routinely fortified with vitamin D to support bone health across the population. One glass (about 250 ml) of fortified milk commonly supplies roughly 2.5 µg (100 IU) of vitamin D plus around 300 mg of calcium. When evaluating products, check label information on vitamin D form (D2 vs D3), total IU per serving, and added sugars.

Plant-based vitamin D drinks: Soy, almond, and oat milks are often fortified to match or exceed the vitamin and mineral profile of cow’s milk. Fortification levels vary; a common range is 2.5–5 µg per serving. Note that many vegan D3 sources are lichen-derived if brands advertise vegan D3. Consumers should review ingredient lists for stabilizers and sweeteners.

Fortified juices and functional waters: Some orange juices and specialty beverages are fortified with vitamin D and calcium, offering a palatable option for those who prefer juice in the morning. These products can help contribute to daily requirements but may contain added sugars.

Natural beverage sources

Very few drinks naturally contain meaningful vitamin D. Bone broths or fish-based broths may have trace amounts, but are not reliable primary sources. UV-treated mushroom powders and certain fortified plant extracts can be added to smoothies as a plant-derived option.

Practical use: smoothies and meal pairing

Smoothies are a flexible strategy for increasing vitamin D intake. Using a fortified milk (dairy or plant-based) as the liquid base and adding healthy fats (avocado, nut butter, flaxseed) improves absorption because vitamin D is fat-soluble. Adding protein, leafy greens, or UV-treated mushroom powder can make a nutrient-dense beverage appropriate for breakfast or a snack.

Evidence-based guidance

Vitamin D3 is generally more effective at raising and maintaining serum 25(OH)D levels than vitamin D2, though D2 remains an acceptable option for vegans when sourced from mushrooms or lichen. Labels in the EU and many other regions are required to provide nutrient content per serving, so choose products with clear vitamin D labeling for dosage control. Combine fortified beverages with dietary sources and sensible sun exposure to maintain optimal status; testing and individualized advice from a healthcare professional are recommended for those at risk of deficiency.

Key takeaways

Fortified milks (dairy and plant-based) and fortified orange juice are the most practical drink-based sources of vitamin D. Check labels for D2 vs D3, IU per serving, and added sugars, and pair fat-soluble vitamin intake with healthy fats to enhance absorption.
