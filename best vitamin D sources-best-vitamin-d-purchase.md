Choosing a supplement begins with understanding forms and sources. Vitamin D in supplements appears mainly as D2 (ergocalciferol) and D3 (cholecalciferol). D3—commonly derived from lanolin or from lichen for vegan formulations—tends to raise and maintain serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25(OH)D) more effectively than D2 in most clinical trials. Delivery format affects convenience and absorption. Oil-based softgels, capsules, and liquid drops generally provide better bioavailability because vitamin D is fat-soluble. Chewables and gummies can increase adherence for children but often include added sugars and lower doses. Sublingual sprays and liposomal formulations may benefit people with malabsorption, though evidence for superiority beyond standard oil-based formulations remains limited.

Dosage should be individualized. General recommendations for most adults range between 600 and 2,000 IU daily, but deficiency correction often requires higher, short-term loading doses under medical supervision. Serum 25(OH)D testing before and during supplementation helps prevent under- or over-supplementation. Individuals with obesity, darker skin, limited sun exposure, or malabsorption may need adjusted regimens. Maximizing absorption is practical: take vitamin D with a meal that contains dietary fat (olive oil, nuts, dairy, or avocado) and consider formulations that specify an oil carrier. Magnesium and vitamin K2 act as cofactors in vitamin D metabolism and calcium handling, so concurrent nutritional status can influence outcomes. If gastrointestinal disease or certain medications affect fat absorption, a healthcare professional can recommend an appropriate formulation or dosing schedule.

Safety considerations are essential. Hypervitaminosis D, though uncommon, can lead to hypercalcemia and related complications; prolonged intakes above the commonly cited upper limit (4,000 IU/day) should be supervised with periodic blood tests. Choose supplements with transparent labeling of dose per serving and third-party testing where available. Neutral product selection criteria include source disclosure (e.g., vegan D3 from lichen), carrier oils, additive-free formulations, and clear dosing instructions.
