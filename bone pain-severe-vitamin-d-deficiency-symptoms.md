Vitamin D, synthesized in skin after sun exposure, is crucial for calcium regulation, bone health, muscle function, immune response and brain chemistry. Severe deficiency can present with multisystem symptoms that are often subtle at first but become more pronounced as stores decline. This article summarizes key signs to help identify when medical evaluation and testing may be warranted.

Bone pain and structural problems

A hallmark of severe deficiency is bone pain and impaired mineralization. Low vitamin D reduces intestinal calcium absorption, prompting the body to resorb calcium from bone. Adults may develop osteomalacia (diffuse bone pain, fractures, difficulty walking), while children can present with rickets (skeletal deformities, delayed growth). Persistent, diffuse aching in weight-bearing areas such as hips, legs and lower back should prompt evaluation.

Muscle weakness and mobility issues

Vitamin D receptors exist in muscle tissue; severe deficiency commonly produces proximal muscle weakness, difficulty rising from chairs, climbing stairs, increased falls and slower gait. In older adults this contributes substantially to frailty. Supplementation in deficient individuals has been associated with improvements in strength and balance in clinical trials.

Chronic fatigue and low energy

Fatigue that does not improve with rest may be related to low vitamin D, which affects cellular energy processes and neuromuscular function. When fatigue co-occurs with muscle aches or mood changes, vitamin D status should be considered among other causes.

Mood changes and cognitive effects

Vitamin D influences neurotransmitter pathways and brain regions involved in mood regulation. Observational studies link low levels to higher rates of depression, seasonal affective disorder and cognitive complaints. While supplementation is not a primary treatment for psychiatric illness, correcting deficiency can be one component of comprehensive care. Impaired immune function

Vitamin D acts as an immunomodulator, supporting innate defenses and regulating inflammatory responses. Severe deficiency is associated with increased susceptibility to respiratory infections and slower recovery from illness. When to test and next steps

Symptoms overlap with many conditions, so serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D testing is the standard way to confirm deficiency. Levels below 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) indicate deficiency; severe deficiency is often defined at lower thresholds. Management should be individualized and may include supervised supplementation and monitoring of calcium, phosphate and alkaline phosphatase.
