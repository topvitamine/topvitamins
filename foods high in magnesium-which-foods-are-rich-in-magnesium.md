Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in over 300 biochemical reactions, including energy production, muscle function, nerve signalling, and bone structure. Many diets fall short of recommended intakes, so prioritizing magnesium-dense foods helps support overall health and can complement targeted supplementation when needed. This concise guide outlines the best natural sources and practical ways to include them in daily meals.

Leafy greens are among the top plant sources. Cooked spinach and Swiss chard provide high amounts of magnesium per serving (often exceeding 100 mg per cooked cup), along with iron, manganese, and fiber. Regularly adding raw or lightly steamed greens to salads, smoothies, or bowls preserves mineral content and increases daily magnesium intake.

Nuts and seeds are compact, portable sources. Pumpkin seeds are particularly magnesium-rich (an ounce can supply a substantial portion of the recommended daily intake). Almonds and cashews also contribute meaningful amounts and pair well with fruit, yogurt, or whole-grain breakfasts. Including a small handful of mixed nuts or a sprinkle of seeds on dishes is an easy way to raise intake.

Whole grains and legumes provide sustained magnesium alongside fiber and plant protein. Quinoa, brown rice, lentils, black beans, and buckwheat are useful staples. Because phytates in some grains and legumes can modestly reduce mineral bioavailability, combine them with vitamin C–rich vegetables (e.g., bell peppers or broccoli) to enhance absorption.

Seafood offers both magnesium and complementary nutrients such as omega-3 fatty acids. Fatty fish like mackerel and salmon, as well as shellfish like shrimp, contribute magnesium while supporting cardiovascular and cognitive health through EPA and DHA.

Certain fruits and cacao-containing foods can also help. Avocado and banana provide moderate magnesium amounts with healthy fats and potassium; dried figs offer concentrated minerals but should be consumed in moderation due to sugars. Dark chocolate and raw cacao are surprisingly rich sources—high-cacao chocolate can be a nutrient-dense treat when chosen with low added sugar.

Other practical options include tofu, edamame, sesame seeds, and fortified plant milks or cereals for those who require alternative sources. Combining several magnesium-rich ingredients—such as a bowl of quinoa topped with roasted tofu, pumpkin seeds, and spinach—produces meals that are both balanced and mineral-dense.

Dietary strategies to maximise magnesium uptake include choosing minimally processed plant foods, preferring steaming or raw preparations over prolonged boiling, and supporting gut health with fiber and probiotic-rich foods to aid absorption. Adults generally need roughly 310–420 mg daily depending on age and sex; individual needs may be higher for athletes, pregnant people, or those with certain medical conditions.
