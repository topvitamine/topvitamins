# Which drink is rich in Vitamin B12?

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is essential for nervous system function, red blood cell production, and DNA synthesis. Because humans cannot synthesize B12, dietary intake or supplementation is required. Drinks can be a practical vector for B12 delivery—either naturally occurring in animal-based beverages or added via fortification. ## Types of B12-rich drinks

1. Fortified plant milks and juices: Many almond, soy, oat, and rice milks are fortified with cyanocobalamin or methylcobalamin. Typical fortification levels often range from about 1.0 to 3.0 mcg per cup, roughly meeting the adult Recommended Daily Intake (RDI) of 2.4 mcg when consumed routinely. Check labels for the form and amount of B12 and for added sugars.

2. Animal-based beverages: Dairy milk, yogurt drinks, and fermented dairy like kefir contain naturally occurring B12. A cup of whole milk provides roughly 1.2 mcg. Seafood broths—especially clam juice—can be exceptionally high in B12, while bone and fish broths contain smaller but meaningful amounts depending on preparation.

3. B12 shots and clinical preparations: Intramuscular or subcutaneous injections of methylcobalamin or cyanocobalamin provide nearly complete bioavailability and are indicated for individuals with malabsorption, pernicious anemia, or severe deficiency. These are administered under medical supervision and are not a first-line option for the general population.

4. Smoothies and blended drinks: Combining fortified plant milk or dairy yogurt with other nutrient-dense ingredients creates a convenient source of B12. Fortified nutritional yeast is another option for vegan smoothies, though not all yeast products supply bioavailable B12.

## Practical considerations

- Bioavailability: Injectable B12 bypasses gastrointestinal absorption and raises serum levels rapidly. Oral and drink-based sources rely on normal digestive function and intrinsic factor for optimal absorption.

- Dietary patterns: Vegans and many vegetarians typically require fortified foods or supplements. Older adults and those on certain medications (e.g., metformin or proton-pump inhibitors) may also have reduced absorption and should monitor status.

- Serving size and labeling: Fortified beverages vary widely. Regular consumption of a cup of fortified plant milk or dairy can meaningfully contribute to daily intake, but consuming a variety of sources improves nutrient security.

## Evidence-based recommendations

When choosing drinks to support B12 status, prioritize verified fortification (clearly labeled mcg per serving) or naturally B12-rich animal products if appropriate for your diet. Clinical interventions such as injections are reserved for confirmed deficiencies or absorption disorders. ## Summary

Drinks can be an effective and convenient part of a strategy to maintain adequate B12 intake. Fortified plant milks are a reliable vegan option, dairy and seafood-based broths supply natural B12 for omnivores, and clinical injections are appropriate for specific medical indications. Monitor intake relative to the RDI (about 2.4 mcg for most adults) and consult a healthcare professional when deficiency or absorption concerns arise.
