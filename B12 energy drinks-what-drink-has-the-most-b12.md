## What Drink Has the Most B12?

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is essential for nerve function, red blood cell production, and energy metabolism. While animal foods are traditional sources, beverages fortified with B12 have emerged as convenient alternatives—particularly for vegans, vegetarians, older adults, and people with malabsorption issues. This article summarizes the main drinkable sources of B12 and practical considerations for choosing them.

### Key drink categories and their B12 content

- Fortified plant milks: Soy, oat, and almond milks are commonly fortified with B12. A typical 240 ml serving contains about 1.5–3.0 mcg (roughly 60–125% of the daily value), making these a reliable daily option when fortification is verified on labels.

- B12 energy drinks: These products span a wide range, from modest fortification to very high doses. Some energy drinks and functional waters offer 10–500 mcg per serving or more; certain formulations marketed as high-potency can list doses in the hundreds to thousands of micrograms. Because forms like methylcobalamin and cyanocobalamin are water-soluble, excess B12 is usually excreted, but ingredient interactions (e.g., caffeine) merit attention.

- Vegan B12 shots: Small concentrated shots (often 1–2 ounces) can contain 500–5,000 mcg of B12 per dose. They use highly bioavailable forms such as methylcobalamin and can be useful for targeted supplementation when rapid repletion or bypassing gastrointestinal malabsorption is desired.

- Dairy-free B12 smoothies and fortified meal shakes: Commercial smoothies with added B12 typically provide 1.5–4 mcg per serving and combine other nutrients like fiber, protein, or probiotics to support overall nutrition.

### Practical considerations

Form matters: Methylcobalamin is often recommended for its bioavailability, while cyanocobalamin is more stable and widely used in fortified foods. Frequency matters more than occasional megadoses for maintenance—regular, labeled intake from fortified beverages or supplements supports steady status.

Read labels: Fortification is not universal—checking the nutrition panel and the specific form of B12 listed is essential. Be mindful of added sugars, artificial additives, and caffeine in energy formulations.

Absorption context: Taking B12 with food can improve uptake in some individuals. Those with conditions like pernicious anemia or gastrointestinal disorders should consult a clinician, as injectable or higher-dose oral strategies may be necessary.

### Comparative insight and resources

For everyday maintenance, fortified plant milks (soy or oat) are practical and diet-friendly. For acute repletion or convenience, vegan B12 shots and high-potency energy shots provide the highest per-serving amounts. For balanced nutrition, fortified smoothies and meal replacement drinks combine B12 with other micronutrients.

### Bottom line

High-dose B12 per serving is most commonly found in vegan B12 shots and some fortified energy drinks, while fortified plant milks and smoothies provide consistent, moderate daily intake. Choose products with clear labeling and the appropriate B12 form for your needs, and consult a healthcare professional when deficiency or absorption concerns exist.
