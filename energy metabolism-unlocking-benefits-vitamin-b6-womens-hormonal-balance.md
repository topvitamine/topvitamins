Introduction

Women’s hormonal health is influenced by nutrition, stress, and lifestyle. Among micronutrients, vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) plays a distinct biochemical role in neurotransmitter synthesis, amino acid metabolism, and hormonal regulation. This article summarizes current evidence on how vitamin B6 can support menstrual health, mood regulation, stress resilience, and energy metabolism while noting practical dosing and safety considerations.

Vitamin B6: biological role and mechanisms

Vitamin B6 functions as a coenzyme in more than 100 enzymatic reactions, including the conversion of tryptophan to serotonin and the synthesis of dopamine and GABA. These pathways connect nutrient status to neuroendocrine control: neurotransmitter availability influences hypothalamic and pituitary signaling that helps regulate reproductive hormones, prolactin, and aspects of estrogen metabolism. By participating in amino acid and one‑carbon metabolism, B6 also supports hepatic hormone conjugation and clearance.

PMS symptom relief and mood regulation

Clinical studies have reported that supplemental vitamin B6 can reduce several premenstrual symptoms, particularly mood‑related complaints and breast tenderness. Meta‑analyses and randomized trials typically assess doses between 25–100 mg/day and observe symptom improvement over one to several cycles. The proposed mechanism is improved serotonin synthesis and modulation of prolactin levels, which together can attenuate irritability, depression, and physical symptoms associated with the luteal phase.

Stress resilience and energy metabolism

Chronic stress elevates cortisol and may disrupt menstrual regularity. Vitamin B6 supports neurotransmitters involved in stress response, which can indirectly promote neuroendocrine stability. In addition, B6 is integral to carbohydrate, fat, and protein metabolism; adequate levels support cellular energy production and hemoglobin synthesis, which can affect vitality and recovery from fatigue.

Practical dosing and safety

For symptomatic support (PMS, mood), commonly studied doses range from 25–100 mg daily, often started in the luteal phase for PMS or taken continuously for mood support. The active coenzyme form, pyridoxal‑5‑phosphate (P5P), may offer improved bioavailability in some individuals. Chronic excessive intake (generally >200 mg/day over long periods) has been associated with sensory neuropathy; therefore, personalized assessment with a healthcare professional is advisable.

Monitoring effectiveness

Evaluate outcomes using symptom tracking across menstrual cycles, mood scales, and fatigue measures. Summary

Vitamin B6 supports neurotransmitter synthesis, contributes to energy metabolism, and can help alleviate certain PMS and mood symptoms, making it a relevant nutrient in strategies aimed at improving hormonal balance.
