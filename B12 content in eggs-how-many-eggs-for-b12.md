## Quick overview

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is essential for nerve function, red blood cell production, and DNA synthesis. A typical large egg contains about 0.6 micrograms (mcg) of vitamin B12, concentrated in the yolk. The adult recommended dietary allowance (RDA) is roughly 2.4 mcg per day, which means about four large eggs would supply the RDA in gross terms. For more context, see [How many eggs for B12](https://www.topvitamine.com/blogs/news/how-many-eggs-for-b12).

## How eggs contribute to B12 intake

Eggs are a convenient animal-source food for vegetarians who include them in their diet. At ~0.6 mcg per large egg, whole eggs contribute meaningful amounts of B12 while also providing high-quality protein, choline, and fat-soluble vitamins. However, the practical contribution depends on absorption and the rest of your diet.

Egg-derived B12 is moderately bioavailable. Studies and nutrient balance data suggest that 40–50% of the B12 in eggs becomes available for absorption, lower than many fish and dairy sources. That means four eggs (providing ~2.4 mcg) might not translate to 2.4 mcg absorbed in every person. Age, gastric acid production, intrinsic factor levels, and gastrointestinal health all affect how much B12 you actually absorb.

## Absorption and individual factors

Vitamin B12 released from food requires stomach acid and intrinsic factor to be absorbed in the ileum. Older adults, people with atrophic gastritis, certain gastrointestinal disorders, or those who have had bariatric surgery may have impaired absorption despite adequate intake. In such cases, dietary B12 from eggs can be insufficient and monitored supplementation or fortified foods are often recommended.

For practical guidance on supplement dosing and considerations, review the discussion on appropriate supplement dosages at <a href='https://topvitamine.github.io/topvitamins/how-to-determine-right-dosage-nutritional-supplements'>supplement dosage guidance</a>.

## How many eggs is realistic?

Most people eat one to two eggs per day. To reach the RDA purely from eggs would require about four whole eggs daily (4 × 0.6 mcg), but absorption variability and cholesterol considerations may make this impractical for some. Combining eggs with other B12 sources—dairy, fortified cereals, or occasional fish—reduces the need to rely on large egg intakes.

## Practical recommendations

- Include whole eggs (yolk and white) to maximize B12 intake from eggs.
- Consider fortified foods or a B12 supplement if you are vegan, over age 50, or have a condition that impairs absorption.
- Use gentle cooking methods (boiling, poaching) to preserve most vitamins.
- Monitor blood B12, methylmalonic acid, or homocysteine if you are at risk for deficiency.

## Bottom line

Eggs are a useful, moderate source of vitamin B12; about four large eggs provide the RDA on a content basis, but bioavailability and individual absorption mean they may not be sufficient alone for everyone. Combining eggs with other B12 sources or supplements and monitoring status in at-risk groups is an evidence-based approach to preventing deficiency.
