Nutritional supplements can support health when used appropriately, but the market contains poor‑quality and counterfeit products. This article outlines common red flags to help you evaluate supplements objectively and reduce risk when choosing products.

Common red flags

- Vague ingredient lists or “proprietary blends” that hide individual dosages. Without clear amounts, you cannot confirm an evidence‑based dose.
- Overstated or medical claims such as “cures,” “instant results,” or “detoxifies everything.” Legitimate supplements avoid promising disease cures.
- Missing or unclear manufacturing and expiration dates, or absent lot/batch numbers that prevent traceability.
- No third‑party testing seals or certificates of analysis (COAs) from recognized organizations.
- Poor packaging quality: blurry labels, spelling errors, inconsistent fonts, or damaged tamper seals are common signs of counterfeits.
- Unusually low price points or sellers using high‑pressure sales tactics (countdown timers, forced urgency).
- Inconsistent product appearance between bottles or odd smells and textures suggesting spoilage or poor manufacturing.

How to verify authenticity and quality

First, read the Supplement Facts and ingredient list closely: look for the exact chemical form (e.g., magnesium citrate vs. magnesium oxide) and the amount in milligrams, micrograms, or IU. Verify claims by checking cited studies directly; credible brands reference peer‑reviewed research rather than unnamed “clinical proof.”

Check for manufacturer traceability: does the label list a physical address, contact information, or a website? Trusted producers often publish batch COAs or provide QR codes linking to batch‑specific test results. Third‑party testing and certifications (e.g., USP, NSF, Informed‑Sport) are important evidence that a product contains what the label claims and is screened for contaminants. When labels lack these verifications, treat them with caution.

Where to buy and what to avoid

Purchase from established retailers or directly from verified brand channels rather than anonymous marketplace third‑party sellers. Steep discounts on high‑demand nutrients can indicate expired, diluted, or counterfeit stock. If available, use on‑package security features—holograms, embossed caps, or QR codes—to confirm authenticity. Storage and safety reminders

Store supplements in a cool, dry place away from humidity and direct sunlight to preserve potency. Check expiry dates before use and discontinue any product that smells rancid or shows visible deterioration. Always consider potential interactions with medications and consult a healthcare professional when in doubt.
