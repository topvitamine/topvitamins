Vitamin D supports bone health, immune function and muscle performance, yet dietary sources are limited. Understanding which foods supply the most vitamin D—and how to combine them with fortified items or supplements—helps maintain adequate status, especially when sunlight exposure is insufficient.

Fatty fish are consistently the richest natural sources. Wild-caught salmon, mackerel and trout deliver high amounts of vitamin D3 per serving alongside omega-3 fatty acids, which offer cardiovascular and anti-inflammatory benefits. Cod liver oil provides a particularly concentrated dose: a tablespoon can supply well over 1,000 IU, though its vitamin A content warrants moderation.

Animal-based foods provide cholecalciferol (D3), the form most effective at raising serum 25(OH)D levels. Eggs contain modest amounts, with higher concentrations in yolks from hens exposed to sunlight or vitamin D–enriched feeds. For plant-based diets, UV-exposed mushrooms produce ergocalciferol (D2); treated varieties can contribute meaningful amounts but may not sustain blood levels as robustly as D3.

Fortified foods play an important role in public health strategies to reduce deficiency. Milk, plant milks (soy, almond, oat), orange juice and many breakfast cereals are commonly fortified with vitamin D; these items reliably augment intake across diverse diets. Pairing vitamin D sources with dietary fat improves absorption because vitamin D is fat-soluble.

Supplements provide a controlled method to correct or maintain vitamin D status when diet and sunlight are inadequate. Cholecalciferol (D3) is generally preferred over ergocalciferol (D2) for its greater potency and longer duration of effect. Dose requirements vary by age, baseline levels and clinical context; typical maintenance intakes for adults range from 600–2,000 IU daily, with higher therapeutic doses used under clinical supervision.

Groups at elevated risk of insufficiency include older adults, people with limited sun exposure, those with malabsorption disorders, and individuals following strict vegan diets. For practical meal planning, integrate fatty fish two to three times weekly, include eggs several times per week, and use fortified beverages or cereals daily if needed. UV-treated mushrooms are a useful plant-based addition. Because absorption improves with fat, prepare vitamin D foods with healthy fats such as olive oil or avocado.
