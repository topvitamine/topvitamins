Introduction

Modern life exposes people to persistent psychological and physiological stressors. Chronic stress can dysregulate sleep, immunity, metabolism and mood through sustained activation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis. Adaptogens are a class of botanicals historically used in Ayurveda and Traditional Chinese Medicine that have attracted scientific interest for their potential to normalize stress responses and support resilience.

What adaptogens are and how they work

Adaptogens are defined by three core properties: a non-specific increase in resistance to stressors, a normalizing influence on physiology, and safety with minimal physiological disruption. Biochemically, many adaptogens appear to modulate HPA axis activity and influence cortisol dynamics, neurotransmitter systems and antioxidant pathways. Evidence-based examples include randomized and controlled trials showing Ashwagandha-associated reductions in serum cortisol and improved perceived stress scores, and studies of Rhodiola demonstrating benefits for fatigue and cognitive performance under stress.

Key adaptogenic herbs and the evidence base

Several herbs recur in clinical and preclinical literature. Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is often studied for anxiolytic effects and cortisol reduction. Rhodiola rosea is associated with enhanced mental endurance and reduced subjective fatigue in stressful contexts. Holy Basil (Tulsi) has been evaluated for mood regulation and adaptive responses to psychosocial stress. Schisandra is noted in Traditional Chinese Medicine for endurance and liver-supportive actions, while Panax ginseng is frequently studied for energy and immune modulation. Controlled studies vary in size and methodology; effect magnitudes depend on extract standardization, dose and duration. A realistic expectation is moderate symptomatic improvement over weeks rather than immediate resolution.
