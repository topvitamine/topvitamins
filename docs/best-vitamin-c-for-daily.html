Introduction

Vitamin C is an essential water-soluble nutrient with antioxidant properties, supporting immune function, collagen synthesis, and iron absorption. Because humans cannot synthesize it, consistent dietary intake or supplementation is necessary. Determining the best vitamin C for daily use depends on absorption (bioavailability), tolerability, formulation, and individual needs.

Forms and Bioavailability

Common supplement formats include ascorbic acid, buffered (mineral ascorbates), Ester-C (calcium ascorbate with metabolites), liposomal vitamin C, and food-based extracts. Ascorbic acid is well studied and cost-effective, but its absorption efficiency declines with large single doses. Liposomal formulations encapsulate vitamin C in phospholipids and often achieve higher plasma levels, while buffered or Ester-C options reduce acidity and can be gentler for sensitive stomachs.

Dosage and Timing Considerations

Recommended daily intakes vary by age and life stage; typical adult reference values are roughly 95–110 mg/day in some regions, with higher therapeutic doses used clinically. Because intestinal uptake becomes less efficient at higher single doses, splitting intake into multiple smaller doses (for example, 250 mg two to three times daily) can maintain steadier plasma levels and reduce gastrointestinal side effects. Buffered or sustained-release products are alternatives for those who experience discomfort.

Formulation matters: liposomal or esterified preparations may be preferred when higher systemic delivery is desired, while food-based supplements supply co-nutrients like flavonoids that can support absorption and antioxidant synergy.

Dietary Sources and Complementary Strategies

Whole foods remain an important source of vitamin C; citrus fruits, bell peppers, kiwi, strawberries, and leafy greens provide ascorbic acid alongside bioflavonoids and fiber. Combining diet and supplementation can be valuable for populations at greater risk of low intake—smokers, older adults, and individuals with limited fresh-food access.
