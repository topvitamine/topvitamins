Can Multivitamins Cause Anxiety? An Evidence-Based Overview

Multivitamins are widely used to address nutrient shortfalls, but concerns sometimes arise about their effects on mood and anxiety. Evidence indicates that while most people tolerate recommended multivitamin doses without psychological side effects, specific nutrients in excess or certain nutrient interactions can produce physiological symptoms that mimic or contribute to anxiety.

How vitamin doses and types matter

Vitamins differ in how the body handles them. Water-soluble vitamins (B-complex and vitamin C) are generally excreted more readily, but high intakes of some B vitamins—especially vitamin B6—have been linked in clinical reports to neurological symptoms, including sensory changes and irritability, when taken at very high doses. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) accumulate in body stores and can produce systemic effects if supra-physiological doses are sustained; for example, vitamin D excess may lead to hypercalcemia and secondary symptoms such as fatigue or confusion that could be mistaken for anxiety.

Mineral balance and mental state

Minerals included in multivitamins—magnesium, zinc, iron, and calcium—play key roles in neurotransmission and neuronal health. Magnesium is commonly associated with calming pathways and low levels have been correlated with increased anxiety in some observational studies. Conversely, excess calcium can interfere with magnesium absorption and has been associated with mood changes. Zinc excess can impair copper status, with downstream effects on cognition and mood. Psychological and behavioral factors

Aside from biochemical mechanisms, psychological phenomena can influence perceived effects. The nocebo effect—expecting negative outcomes—can produce or amplify anxiety-like sensations after starting a new supplement. Additionally, overlapping intake from fortified foods and multiple supplements can unknowingly raise total micronutrient exposure, increasing the risk of side effects and feeding back into health-related worry.

Interactions, vulnerable groups, and quality concerns

Drug–nutrient interactions and product quality are important considerations. Certain supplements can alter the absorption or effectiveness of medications; vulnerable populations such as pregnant women, children, and older adults require tailored guidance. Contaminants or undeclared ingredients in poorly regulated products may provoke adverse reactions. Practical guidance

Is there a direct, universal link between multivitamins and anxiety? Current evidence does not support a blanket causal relationship for most users taking recommended doses. However, individual sensitivity, excessive intake, mineral imbalances, interactions with medications, and psychological factors can all contribute to anxiety-like symptoms in some people.
