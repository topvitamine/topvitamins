Introduction

Dizziness and vertigo are common complaints with many possible causes. Recent research has explored whether vitamin D deficiency might contribute to balance disturbances, either directly through inner ear effects or indirectly via muscle weakness, impaired proprioception, and neurological changes. This article summarizes current evidence and practical considerations for clinicians and individuals worried about unexplained dizziness.

Vitamin D: roles relevant to balance

Vitamin D regulates calcium and phosphate homeostasis, supports muscle function, and influences neuronal health. Receptors for vitamin D are present in skeletal muscle and in parts of the central nervous system involved in sensory integration. These biological roles provide plausible pathways by which low vitamin D status could affect postural control and spatial orientation.

What the evidence shows

Observational studies have reported associations between lower serum 25(OH)D levels and higher rates of falls, impaired gait, and recurrent benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV). Randomized trials focused on fall prevention indicate modest improvements in muscle strength and reduced fall risk with vitamin D repletion in older adults, although results vary by dose and baseline status. A number of clinical reports also note that patients with recurrent BPPV frequently have lower vitamin D levels compared with controls.

Proposed mechanisms

Several mechanisms could link vitamin D deficiency with dizziness:

  • Inner ear (otoconia) integrity: vitamin D influences calcium metabolism within the vestibular organs, and deficiency may contribute to dislodgement of otoliths implicated in BPPV.
  • Muscle function and proprioception: insufficient vitamin D impairs muscle contraction and neuromuscular signaling, reducing the ability to correct posture during perturbations.
  • Neurological effects: vitamin D has neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory actions and affects neurotransmitters involved in sensory processing; deficiency could therefore alter central compensation for balance disturbances.

Clinical considerations

Dizziness is multifactorial. Vitamin D deficiency is neither necessary nor sufficient to cause dizziness in every case, but it can be a contributing factor—especially when dizziness coexists with muscle weakness, fatigue, bone pain, or recurrent BPPV. Measurement of serum 25(OH)D is the standard way to assess status. Management decisions should be individualized, taking into account age, comorbidities, and concurrent medications that affect vitamin D metabolism.

Conclusion

Vitamin D deficiency may contribute to dizziness through multiple biologically plausible pathways, particularly in older adults and people with recurrent vestibular symptoms. Assessment of vitamin D status can be considered when evaluating unexplained dizziness, but it should complement—not replace—a comprehensive diagnostic workup.
