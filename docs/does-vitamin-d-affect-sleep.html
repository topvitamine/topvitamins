Does vitamin D affect sleep?

Sleep quality depends on interconnected biological systems, and emerging research suggests vitamin D may be one contributing factor. Traditionally known for bone health and calcium regulation, vitamin D interacts with receptors in the brain, immune system pathways, and circadian-related gene expression—mechanisms that could influence sleep duration, latency, and sleep fragmentation.

Evidence linking vitamin D and sleep

Observational studies report associations between low serum 25(OH)D levels and poorer sleep outcomes, including shorter total sleep time, increased awakenings, and greater daytime sleepiness. Meta-analyses and several randomized trials have offered mixed but generally suggestive results: some trials show modest improvements in sleep efficiency and duration after vitamin D3 supplementation, while others find no significant change. Differences in baseline vitamin D status, dose, duration, and participant characteristics likely explain much of the variability.

Biological plausibility

There are several plausible pathways through which vitamin D could affect sleep. Vitamin D receptors and metabolizing enzymes are present in brain areas involved in sleep regulation, such as the hypothalamus. Vitamin D influences inflammatory cytokines; since systemic inflammation is known to disrupt sleep, correcting deficiency could reduce inflammation-related sleep disturbance. Additionally, vitamin D may modulate the serotonin-melatonin pathway by affecting enzymes involved in serotonin synthesis, indirectly supporting nighttime melatonin production and normal sleep onset.

Circadian rhythms and light

Natural light exposure is central to circadian entrainment and also drives skin synthesis of vitamin D. Reduced daylight exposure, especially in winter or in indoor lifestyles, can therefore compromise both circadian cues and vitamin D status. Morning sunlight supports cortisol rhythms and suppresses melatonin during the day, helping consolidate nighttime sleep. Clinical considerations

Vitamin D deficiency—commonly defined as 25(OH)D below 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L)—is associated with fatigue and may exacerbate insomnia and fragmented sleep. Clinicians often consider testing vitamin D status as part of an evaluation for chronic sleep complaints, particularly when other signs of deficiency are present. Supplementation decisions should be individualized; common maintenance doses range from 600–2000 IU daily depending on age, baseline levels, and clinical context. Practical takeaways

Current evidence supports an association between vitamin D status and sleep health, but causality is not fully established. Addressing deficiency through sensible sun exposure, diet, and monitored supplementation can be part of a multifaceted approach to improving sleep, alongside sleep hygiene and management of underlying conditions.
