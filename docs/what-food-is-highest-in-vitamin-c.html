What food is highest in vitamin C?

Introduction

Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is a water-soluble micronutrient required for collagen synthesis, antioxidant defense, iron absorption, and immune function. Because the body does not store vitamin C for long periods, regular dietary intake is important to prevent deficiency and support tissue repair and antioxidant protection.

Top natural sources

Fruits and vegetables vary widely in vitamin C content. Among common foods, guava stands out as exceptionally rich in vitamin C, providing roughly 228 mg per 100 g, which exceeds typical daily requirements by a wide margin. Other high-percentage sources include red bell peppers (about 190 mg per cup chopped), kiwi (around 70 mg per medium fruit), and several leafy greens such as raw kale (approximately 80 mg per cup).

Practical considerations

Vitamin C is heat-sensitive and water-soluble, so preparation methods influence retained content. Steaming or quick sautéing preserves more vitamin C than boiling; consuming many fruits and vegetables raw also maximizes intake. Frozen produce can retain much of its vitamin C when blanched and frozen promptly, making it a reliable option when fresh items are out of season.

Food versus supplementation

Whole foods supply vitamin C alongside fiber, flavonoids, carotenoids, and other cofactors that may enhance absorption and provide complementary health effects. Supplements offer precise dosing and convenience when dietary variety is limited or when specific medical or lifestyle needs require higher intake. Evidence suggests smaller, divided doses of supplemental vitamin C are absorbed more efficiently than single very large doses.

Conclusion

Guava, red bell peppers, kiwi, and several leafy greens are among the highest natural sources of vitamin C. Prioritizing these foods within a varied diet helps supply vitamin C along with additional phytonutrients. Where dietary intake is insufficient, supplements can provide consistent dosing, but whole foods remain the preferred foundation for nutrient intake.
