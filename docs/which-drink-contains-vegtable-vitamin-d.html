Which drink has a lot of vitamin D?

Vitamin D is a fat-soluble nutrient essential for calcium absorption, bone health, and immune function. While sunlight is the primary natural source, dietary intake matters for people with limited sun exposure, darker skin, older age, or geographic constraints. Drinks can be a practical way to boost vitamin D intake when they are fortified or made from naturally rich ingredients.

Common vitamin D-rich drinks

Fortified cow's milk and many plant-based milks (soy, almond, oat) are among the most reliable beverage sources of vitamin D in everyday diets. Fortified orange juice is another commonly available option. Typical fortification levels range from about 2.5–5 µg (100–200 IU) per serving depending on the product and local regulations. Check labels for exact amounts and the form of vitamin D used (D2 or D3).

Plant-based options and considerations

For vegans and those who are lactose-intolerant, fortified plant milks offer practicality and nutrient parity when brands add vitamin D alongside calcium and sometimes vitamin B12. Some plant-based products use vitamin D2 (ergocalciferol) while others use vegan D3 derived from lichen; D3 is generally considered more bioavailable in raising serum 25(OH)D levels. For an overview of fortified plant-based drinks see this discussion of plant-based vitamin D drinks.

Fortified juices and specialty beverages

Fortified orange juice can provide both vitamin D and calcium, making it a convenient morning choice. Some functional waters and ready-to-drink beverages are fortified as well, though sugar and caloric content should be considered. When possible, choose unsweetened or low-sugar versions to avoid excess added sugars.

Smoothies and homemade options

Smoothies are a flexible way to increase vitamin D intake: use fortified milk or yogurt as a base, add healthy fats (avocado, nut butter) to support absorption, and consider UV-treated mushroom powder or a measured vitamin D liquid drop if needed. Homemade blends allow control of sugar and macronutrients while integrating vitamin D into regular meals.

Practical guidance

When selecting a drink, read the nutrition label for the amount of vitamin D per serving and whether D2 or D3 is used. Consider calcium content too, since vitamin D facilitates calcium absorption. Key takeaways

  • Fortified milks (dairy and plant-based) and fortified orange juice are the most accessible vitamin D drinks.
  • Check labels for dosage, sugar content, and D2 vs D3 form.
  • Smoothies using fortified bases and healthy fats offer a customizable approach.
  • Combine dietary sources with sensible sun exposure and, if necessary, supplemental vitamin D under clinical advice.
