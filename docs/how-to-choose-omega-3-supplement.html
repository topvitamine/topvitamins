How to Choose an Omega-3 Supplement

Omega-3 fatty acids—primarily EPA (eicosapentaenoic acid) and DHA (docosahexaenoic acid)—play established roles in cardiovascular, neurological, and visual health. Selecting an effective supplement requires attention to source, concentration, purity, and the EPA:DHA ratio most appropriate for your goals.

Form and source

Omega-3s come in fish oil, krill oil, and algae oil formulations, plus plant oils rich in ALA (alpha-linolenic acid). Fish and algae oils provide preformed EPA and DHA; ALA requires metabolic conversion to those active forms and converts inefficiently in many people. Softgels are convenient and often enteric-coated to reduce aftertaste; liquids can deliver higher doses and are useful when therapeutic levels are desired.

EPA vs. DHA: matching ratio to goals

EPA and DHA have overlapping but distinct functions. EPA is more often associated with anti-inflammatory effects and some mood-related outcomes, while DHA is a major structural fatty acid in the brain and retina and is prioritized during pregnancy and early development. For general maintenance, many experts recommend combined EPA+DHA of 250–500 mg daily; for specific therapeutic aims (e.g., elevated triglycerides or certain mood conditions), higher and targeted EPA:DHA ratios may be suggested by a clinician.

Purity, testing, and formulation

Because marine oils can accumulate contaminants, choose products that provide third-party testing and clear labeling of EPA and DHA per serving. Independent certifications from recognized programs and documentation of molecular distillation or equivalent purification methods help reduce exposure to heavy metals, PCBs, and other impurities. Triglyceride or re-esterified triglyceride forms are generally better absorbed than ethyl ester forms, and taking omega-3s with a fat-containing meal improves bioavailability.

Vegan and sustainability considerations

Algae-derived omega-3 supplements supply DHA and increasingly EPA in a plant-based format, avoiding marine contaminants and reducing pressure on fish stocks. Algal oils are an appropriate alternative for those avoiding animal products or seeking lower environmental impact.

Practical tips

  • Read the Supplement Facts to confirm EPA and DHA amounts per serving rather than relying on total fish oil volume.
  • Look for batch-specific test results or certification logos and information on purification methods.
  • Match the EPA:DHA balance to your health focus—higher EPA for inflammation and mood, higher DHA for brain and eye development.
  • Store liquid formulations as instructed to minimize oxidation; many benefit from refrigeration after opening.
  • Discuss appropriate dosing and potential interactions (for example, with anticoagulants) with a healthcare professional.
