Introduction

When people ask “what is the name of the supplement that has all the vitamins?” they are usually referring to a comprehensive multivitamin or an all-in-one vitamin capsule. These products are formulated to provide a broad spectrum of essential vitamins and, commonly, supportive minerals and co-factors so that daily nutritional gaps can be reduced without multiple separate products.

What qualifies as an “all vitamins” supplement?

A truly complete formula will contain the 13 essential vitamins (A, C, D, E, K, and the B‑complex family) and include key minerals such as magnesium, zinc, selenium, iodine and iron when appropriate. It will list bioavailable forms (for example, methylcobalamin for B12, cholecalciferol for D3, and chelated minerals), clear dosing that aligns with safety guidance, and transparent ingredient sourcing. Formats and targeted formulations

Complete supplements are available as tablets, capsules, gummies, powders and liposomal liquids. Some are general one-a-day multis, while others are targeted: prenatal multivitamins prioritize folate and iron; senior formulations emphasize vitamin D, B12 and bone-supportive nutrients; athlete-oriented products may include higher magnesium and B-vitamin support. Choosing the right format depends on absorption needs, convenience and individual requirements.

Evidence-based selection criteria

When evaluating options, consider: nutrient completeness (all essential vitamins present), forms with known bioavailability, dosages within established safety limits, and third-party testing for purity. Limitations and safety

Multivitamins are intended to complement—not replace—a balanced diet. High-dose single nutrients can cause imbalance or interact with medications; for instance, excess iron or vitamin K may be contraindicated in certain conditions. Consulting a healthcare professional for personalized advice and blood testing where relevant is prudent.

Practical advice

Look for clear labels, recommended daily intakes, and independent verification (GMP, ISO or third-party lab seals). Prioritize products without unnecessary additives and consider bioavailability-enhancing pairings (e.g., vitamin D3 with K2, vitamin C to aid iron absorption). Conclusion

There is no single universally trademarked product called “the supplement with all the vitamins,” but comprehensive multivitamin complexes and all-in-one capsules meet that description when they include the full set of essential vitamins, supportive minerals and transparent, bioavailable forms. Evaluate formulations with evidence-based criteria and professional guidance to find the best match for your needs.
