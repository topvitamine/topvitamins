Introduction

Omega-3 fatty acids are essential polyunsaturated fats that support multiple physiological systems, including the cardiovascular and nervous systems. Because the body cannot synthesize sufficient amounts of key omega-3s—EPA and DHA—these nutrients must be obtained from the diet or supplementation. The following summary explains the primary evidence-based roles Omega-3s play in health and practical considerations for intake.

Key functions and dietary sources

Omega-3s include ALA (plant-based) and the marine-derived EPA and DHA. ALA is found in flaxseed, chia, and walnuts, while EPA and DHA are abundant in fatty fish and algal oils. Conversion of ALA to EPA and DHA in humans is limited, so direct dietary or supplemental EPA/DHA is often recommended to achieve physiological effects.

Heart and cardiovascular wellness

Clinical studies indicate that EPA and DHA contribute to cardiovascular health by lowering triglycerides, modestly affecting blood pressure, and improving endothelial function. Regular intake of combined EPA/DHA at preventive doses (for example, around 250–1000 mg daily) is associated with better vascular compliance and reduced markers of cardiovascular risk. Brain function and mental health

DHA is a major structural lipid in the brain and supports membrane fluidity, neurotransmission, and development. Observational and randomized studies link higher EPA/DHA status with improved cognitive performance and mood regulation. Some trials show EPA-rich formulations can help reduce depressive symptoms when used alongside standard care, though results vary and clinical supervision is advised.

Anti-inflammatory effects and immune modulation

EPA and DHA are precursors to specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs) that help resolve inflammation without broadly suppressing immune function. Higher omega-3 intake or blood levels correlate with lower inflammatory biomarkers such as CRP. Therapeutic omega-3 doses for inflammatory conditions are typically higher and should be managed with a clinician.

Joint support and musculoskeletal health

For people with inflammatory joint conditions, omega-3 supplementation can reduce joint pain and morning stiffness and may decrease reliance on anti-inflammatory medications. Benefits often become apparent after several weeks of consistent use and are enhanced when combined with a balanced nutrient approach.

Context and practical guidance

Incorporating omega-3s into a daily nutritional plan can be done through diet (fatty fish, algal sources) or standardized supplements.
