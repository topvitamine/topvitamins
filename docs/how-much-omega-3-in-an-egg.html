How much omega-3 is in an egg?

Eggs are a convenient whole-food source of nutrients, and their omega-3 content varies widely depending on hen diet and production methods. A standard large egg typically contains about 30–70 mg of total omega-3 fatty acids—mainly alpha-linolenic acid (ALA). By contrast, purposefully enriched eggs commonly provide between 300 and 500 mg of combined omega-3s per egg, including measurable amounts of docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and small amounts of eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA).

What determines omega-3 levels?

The primary determinant of egg omega-3 content is what hens eat. Diets supplemented with flaxseed, chia, algae, or fish oil raise the ALA, DHA and EPA levels in yolks. Farming practices also matter: pasture-raised hens that consume fresh forage and insects often produce eggs with higher ALA than conventionally fed hens. Breed, age, and seasonal forage availability can cause further variation. Proper storage avoids oxidation of unsaturated fats, but it does not create or destroy substantial amounts of omega-3s once the egg is formed.

How this compares to dietary recommendations

Health authorities such as the European Food Safety Authority recommend approximately 250 mg/day of combined EPA+DHA for general cardiovascular and cognitive support in adults. A conventional egg alone provides only a small fraction of that recommendation, whereas one enriched egg with 300–500 mg total omega-3s (often including 100–200 mg DHA) can contribute significantly toward recommended DHA+EPA intake. For many people, consuming one to three enriched eggs per day is a food-based strategy to support habitual omega-3 intake without relying solely on supplements or frequent fish consumption.

Practical considerations

When selecting eggs for their omega-3 benefit, look for clear labeling such as “omega-3 enriched,” “DHA,” or descriptions of the hens’ feed (e.g., flax-fed or algae-supplemented). If you use eggs along with other strategies—dietary fish, algae-based supplements, or oral formulations—consider differences in concentration and dosing. Eggs in a broader nutrition context

Eggs provide additional nutrients—about 6 g protein, vitamin D, B12, selenium and choline—so enriched eggs can be an efficient, multi-nutrient option. Summary

In short, ordinary eggs contain modest amounts of omega-3s (≈30–70 mg), while omega-3 enriched eggs can supply roughly 300–500 mg per egg with appreciable DHA. Choosing labeled enriched eggs and combining them with a varied diet offers a practical, evidence-informed way to increase dietary omega-3 intake.
