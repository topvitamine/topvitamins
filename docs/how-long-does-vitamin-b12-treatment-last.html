Introduction

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is central to nerve function, red blood cell production and DNA synthesis. Deficiency is common in older adults, people with restrictive diets, and those with gastrointestinal disorders. Treatment approaches include oral, sublingual, intranasal and intramuscular routes. Understanding how long B12 treatment lasts helps set expectations for symptom recovery and monitoring.

Forms of supplementation and duration

Oral and sublingual supplements require consistent daily or weekly intake and are effective when absorption is intact. Intranasal sprays are used for maintenance in some protocols. Intramuscular injections bypass the gut and are commonly used for severe deficiency or absorption problems; a typical 1,000 µg injection can support levels for several weeks to a few months depending on individual factors.

Factors determining treatment length

Treatment duration depends on the underlying cause: dietary insufficiency often requires a limited course of supplementation until stores are restored, whereas conditions that impair absorption—such as pernicious anemia or certain gastric surgeries—may necessitate lifelong replacement. Other factors include age, metabolic rate, severity of deficiency, and concurrent medications like metformin or proton pump inhibitors that can reduce B12 uptake.

Typical timelines and recovery

Symptom improvement is often reported within days to a week for fatigue and cognitive fog. Hematologic parameters typically show correction within 1 month; neurological recovery may continue over months and can be incomplete if treatment is delayed. A common approach is an initial loading phase (daily or weekly injections for several weeks) followed by maintenance (monthly injections or ongoing oral dosing). Clinical and laboratory reassessments (serum B12, methylmalonic acid, homocysteine) guide adjustments.

Practical considerations and monitoring

Long-term management should balance efficacy with convenience and monitoring. Many people start with injections and switch to oral supplementation once levels normalize, particularly when the deficiency stems from diet rather than malabsorption. Populations at risk—older adults and vegans—benefit from regular monitoring and preventive supplementation. Key takeaways

  • Duration of B12 therapy ranges from weeks to lifelong depending on cause.
  • Injections provide rapid repletion and can last weeks to months; oral forms require regular dosing.
  • Improvement begins quickly for many symptoms, but full recovery can take months.
  • Regular laboratory monitoring and clinical follow-up are essential to tailor therapy.
