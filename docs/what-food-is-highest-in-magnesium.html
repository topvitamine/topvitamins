What food is highest in magnesium?

Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in hundreds of enzymatic reactions, including energy production, muscle contraction, nerve function, and bone maintenance. Many population studies indicate that a substantial portion of adults do not meet recommended magnesium intakes, often because of diets high in processed foods. Prioritizing magnesium-rich whole foods can improve nutrient status while providing complementary vitamins, fiber, and antioxidants.

Top natural sources of magnesium

Certain plant-based foods and some animal products consistently rank highest for magnesium per serving. Seeds and nuts, leafy greens, whole grains, legumes, and dark chocolate are among the most concentrated dietary sources:

  • Pumpkin seeds: One of the richest sources per ounce; they provide a substantial portion of the daily requirement and are convenient for snacking or adding to salads.
  • Leafy greens: Cooked spinach, Swiss chard, and kale offer meaningful magnesium along with vitamin K, iron, and phytonutrients.
  • Nuts: Almonds and cashews supply magnesium in a portable form and also contribute healthy fats and protein.
  • Legumes: Beans, lentils, and soy products (edamame, tempeh) are valuable for both magnesium and fiber.
  • Whole grains: Quinoa, brown rice, and oats retain magnesium that is lost in refined grains.
  • Dark chocolate: Higher-cocoa dark chocolate contains appreciable magnesium in addition to flavonoids.
  • Fatty fish and dairy: These provide smaller amounts of magnesium alongside other nutrients such as omega-3s or calcium.

Optimizing intake and absorption

Dietary patterns influence how well magnesium is absorbed. Phytates in whole grains and legumes can modestly reduce absorption, but traditional preparation methods (soaking, fermenting) and a mixed diet high in vegetables often mitigate this effect. Vitamin D status may also interact with mineral metabolism; for considerations about combined micronutrient strategies, see evidence on taking vitamin D and B12 together.

Sleep, stress, and certain medications can affect magnesium requirements. Practical recommendations

To increase magnesium intake, aim to include a variety of the foods above across meals and snacks: add seeds to yogurt or salads, choose whole grains, include legumes regularly, and incorporate leafy greens cooked or raw. For those with restricted diets or medical conditions that impair absorption, dietary assessment and targeted guidance from a healthcare professional are appropriate.
