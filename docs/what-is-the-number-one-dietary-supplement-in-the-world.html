Introduction

Dietary supplements play a prominent role in contemporary public health strategies, filling nutritional gaps and supporting specific physiological needs. Determining the "number one" supplement globally requires considering prevalence of use, accessibility, evidence of benefit, and role in preventing deficiencies. Synthesizing market data and clinical rationale points to one clear leader: the multivitamin (vitamin complex).

Why multivitamins lead

Multivitamins are the most widely consumed category of dietary supplements worldwide. Their ubiquity is driven by a convergence of factors: broad nutritional coverage, low cost relative to many niche products, and straightforward dosing that appeals to diverse populations from children to older adults. Epidemiological surveys repeatedly show multivitamin use across age groups and regions as people seek a practical way to reduce risk of common micronutrient shortfalls.

Evidence and clinical perspective

Clinical and public health literature frames multivitamins as a pragmatic intervention for bridging dietary insufficiencies. While randomized trials show mixed results for disease prevention in well-nourished populations, consistent findings support their role in preventing frank deficiencies—examples include folic acid in pregnancy to reduce neural tube defects and vitamin D or B12 supplementation where deficiency is prevalent. Multivitamins’ market dominance is evident in global sales and product variety. Formulations tailored to life stages and dietary preferences (e.g., prenatal, senior, vegan) increase relevance and uptake. Practical considerations and limitations

Multivitamins are not a substitute for a balanced diet. Their value is greatest when used to address clear dietary gaps or specific physiological demands. Overuse or high-dose single-nutrient supplementation without medical indication can carry risks, so evidence-based dosing and periodic reassessment are important. Conclusion

Considering prevalence, practical utility, and public health impact, multivitamins emerge as the number one dietary supplement globally. They offer a broad-spectrum approach to micronutrient support, particularly valuable where diet alone may be insufficient.
