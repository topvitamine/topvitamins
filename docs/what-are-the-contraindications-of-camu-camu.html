Introduction

Camu Camu (Myrciaria dubia) is an Amazonian berry prized for its high vitamin C concentration and polyphenols. While it can support antioxidant defenses and immune function, concentrated Camu Camu extracts carry contraindications and safety considerations that merit attention—especially for people with pre-existing conditions, those taking medications, pregnant or breastfeeding individuals, and children.

Key contraindications

Contraindications for Camu Camu primarily relate to its vitamin C potency and potential effects on mineral metabolism and renal function. Groups who should exercise caution include:

  • Pregnant and breastfeeding women: Routine diets normally supply recommended vitamin C levels; high supplemental amounts can exceed guidelines and should be reviewed with a clinician.
  • People with kidney disorders: Excess vitamin C can convert to oxalate and increase the risk of oxalate kidney stones, particularly in those with chronic kidney disease or recurrent stones.
  • Individuals with iron overload (e.g., hemochromatosis): Vitamin C enhances non-heme iron absorption and may worsen iron accumulation.
  • Children: High-potency supplements are generally unnecessary and may exceed age-appropriate upper limits.
  • Patients on certain medications: Those receiving anticoagulants, some chemotherapies, or drugs with narrow therapeutic windows should consult a healthcare provider before adding high-dose vitamin C sources.

Safety, adverse effects, and intolerance

Common adverse effects of excessive Camu Camu intake are gastrointestinal: diarrhea, abdominal cramps, nausea, and acid reflux. Skin irritation or rashes have been reported rarely and may reflect sensitivity to specific plant compounds. Intolerance—non-immune reactions such as bloating or loose stools—can occur, particularly in people with irritable bowel syndrome or histamine sensitivity.

Quality control is also a safety issue; poorly sourced supplements may contain contaminants. Prefer products with third-party testing where possible.

Dosage warnings and practical guidance

There is no standardized therapeutic dose for Camu Camu, but vitamin C upper intake is commonly cited as 2,000 mg/day for adults. Many extracts provide varying vitamin C equivalents; starting with a low dose (for example, a single moderate serving or 250–500 mg extract equivalent) and monitoring tolerance is prudent. Special populations (pregnancy, lactation, children) should adhere to age- and condition-specific recommendations and avoid unsupervised megadoses.

Drug interactions

Vitamin C can interact with anticoagulants, certain chemotherapeutic agents, and drugs whose metabolism or effect is sensitive to redox changes. It also increases iron absorption, which can magnify iron supplementation effects. When in doubt, review supplement plans with a pharmacist or clinician.
