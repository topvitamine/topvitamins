Which yogurt contains the most vitamin B12?

Why vitamin B12 in yogurt matters

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is essential for red blood cell formation, neurological function, and DNA synthesis. The adult recommended intake is about 2.4 µg per day. Because B12 occurs naturally in animal-derived foods, yogurt can be a practical dietary source for omnivores and lacto-vegetarians, while fortified plant-based yogurts can help vegans and vegetarians meet requirements.

Dairy yogurts: natural sources and typical amounts

Traditional dairy yogurts contain natural B12 derived from the milk used. Plain whole-milk yogurt typically supplies roughly 0.6–1.4 µg per 150 g serving, depending on milk type and processing. Greek-style varieties may show slightly lower B12 because straining removes whey where some B12 resides; Icelandic skyr often provides comparable amounts to regular yogurts. Factors such as the animal's diet, regional milk fortification policies, and whether manufacturers add extra B12 can influence final content.

Plant-based yogurts: fortification is key

Plant-based yogurts (soy, almond, coconut, oat, cashew) do not contain meaningful natural B12. Manufacturers commonly add synthetic B12—cyanocobalamin or methylcobalamin—to fortified products. Fortified servings can range from about 0.75 µg to 3 µg per portion. Soy and almond formulations are among the most frequently fortified; coconut and oat products vary more. To rely on plant-based yogurt as a B12 source, check for explicit fortification claims and the nutrition label showing the microgram amount per serving.

Probiotics and B12 absorption

Yogurts with live cultures (e.g., Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains) support gut health and may assist nutrient absorption. Current evidence suggests probiotics can improve gastrointestinal conditions that impair absorption, which indirectly helps B12 status; however, most common probiotic strains do not produce clinically meaningful B12 in the human gut. Choosing a yogurt with live and active cultures may therefore complement dietary B12, particularly for older adults or those with absorption challenges.

Practical guidance: choosing the highest-B12 yogurt

To maximize B12 intake from yogurt: prefer products that list vitamin B12 on the nutrition panel and supply at least ~1 µg per serving; for plant-based eaters, select explicitly fortified options; for dairy consumers, compare plain whole-milk and skyr/Greek varieties and account for serving size.
