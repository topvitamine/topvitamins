## Introduction

Vitamin D is essential for bone health, immune function, and muscle performance. Endogenous synthesis from UVB exposure is the primary source for many people, but geographic location, season, skin coverage, and sunscreen use can limit production. Dietary and beverage sources therefore play an important supporting role in maintaining adequate vitamin D status.

This article summarizes common drinks that contain vitamin D, explains differences between natural and fortified sources, and points to related evidence for nutrient interactions.

## Fortified beverages

Fortification is the most reliable way drinks provide vitamin D. Cow’s milk is frequently fortified in many countries to supply consistent amounts of vitamin D per serving. Likewise, widely consumed plant-based milks—such as soy, almond, oat, rice, and coconut—are commonly enriched during processing. Orange juice and some ready-to-drink breakfast beverages and protein shakes are also often fortified.

When evaluating fortified drinks, check product labels for the form of vitamin D (D2 versus D3) and the amount per serving. Research generally indicates vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol) is more effective at raising serum 25(OH)D than D2 (ergocalciferol), but vegan D3 from lichen is becoming available.

## Plant-based and vegan-friendly options

Fortified plant milks can be an effective strategy for individuals following vegan or lactose-free diets. Many manufacturers pair vitamin D with calcium and vitamin B12 to better match the micronutrient profile of dairy. If following a strict vegan diet, look for products that specify vegan D3 (from lichen) or D2 from fungal sources.

## Natural beverage sources

A limited set of natural drinks contain vitamin D. Bone broths and fish-based broths (including traditional dashi) can provide small amounts of vitamin D3. Emulsified cod liver oil tonics are a concentrated natural source of vitamins D and A, though they are not commonly used as everyday beverages in all populations.

Mushroom teas made from UV-exposed varieties (e.g., maitake or shiitake) deliver vitamin D2 when the fungi have been irradiated with UV light; these are among the few vegan natural sources of vitamin D found in beverages.

## Smoothies and functional formulations

Smoothies can be tailored to increase vitamin D intake by using fortified plant milk or orange juice as a base and including ingredients that enhance fat-soluble vitamin absorption (e.g., avocado or nut butters). Some manufacturers and formulators are also developing sunlight-inspired, nanoemulsion or lichen-D3 infused beverages intended to improve bioavailability.

## Conclusion

Drinks can meaningfully contribute to vitamin D intake, particularly when fortified or when incorporating UV-exposed mushrooms or fish-based broths. Always inspect labels for the form and amount of vitamin D, and consider dietary patterns, sun exposure, and testing when assessing needs.
