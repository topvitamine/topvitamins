## Recommendation for a Good All-Around Multivitamin

Multivitamins can help fill dietary gaps and support daily physiological functions such as energy metabolism, immune response, and cellular repair. They are not a substitute for varied whole foods but can be a practical complement when food intake, absorption, or variety is limited. This article summarizes evidence-based criteria to choose an effective, balanced multivitamin and points to additional resources for informed decision-making.

### What to prioritize in an all-around multivitamin

- Balanced doses: Aim for formulations that provide vitamins and minerals at or near recommended daily allowances (RDAs) rather than megadoses. Excess of certain nutrients (for example, preformed vitamin A or iron when not deficient) can carry risks.
- Bioavailable forms: Nutrient chemical forms affect absorption. Examples include methylfolate instead of folic acid for people with MTHFR variants, and magnesium citrate rather than magnesium oxide for better uptake.
- Third-party testing and purity: Choose products tested for contaminants and accurate labeling. Independent verification reduces the chance of heavy metal contamination or inconsistent potency.
- Synergy and timing: Some nutrients are best taken together (vitamin D with calcium; fat-soluble vitamins with meals) while others may compete for absorption. A once-daily formulation taken with a meal maximizes adherence and uptake.

### Key nutrients to look for

A comprehensive formula typically includes vitamins A, C, D, E, a B-complex (B1, B2, B3, B6, B12, folate), magnesium, zinc, selenium, and often trace elements like copper and manganese. For cognitive or cardiovascular support, omega-3 DHA and low-dose vitamin K variants may be considered alongside standard micronutrients.

### When a multivitamin is particularly useful

Multivitamins are most helpful if your diet is restrictive (e.g., vegan, elimination diets), if you have increased needs (pregnancy, older age, high training loads), or if tests indicate suboptimal levels. ### Practical guidance

- Start with a broad-spectrum, balanced formula taken daily with a meal.
- Avoid unnecessary megadoses and overlapping supplements unless recommended by a clinician.
- If you have medical conditions, are pregnant, lactating, or on medication, consult a healthcare provider before starting supplementation.
