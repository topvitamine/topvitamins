Introduction

Vitamin D is essential for calcium and phosphorus balance, immune regulation, muscle function, and brain health. Deficiency can develop gradually and present with a range of symptoms that are often nonspecific, which can delay diagnosis. Recognizing common signs—bone pain, fatigue, muscle weakness, mood changes, and increased infections—helps guide testing and management.

Common symptoms

- Bone pain and tenderness: Low vitamin D impairs calcium absorption, which can lead to bone demineralization, osteomalacia in adults, and rickets in children. Patients commonly report aching in the lower back, hips, ribs, and legs that is diffuse rather than localized to an acute injury.

- Fatigue and low energy: Several studies associate low serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D with persistent fatigue and reduced vitality. When cellular energy metabolism is affected, people may experience brain fog, daytime sleepiness, and slower recovery after activity.

- Muscle weakness and falls: Vitamin D contributes to muscle contraction and balance. Deficiency can cause proximal muscle weakness (difficulty rising from a chair or climbing stairs) and, in older adults, an increased fall risk.

- Mood changes and cognitive effects: Evidence links vitamin D status with mood regulation and some cognitive functions. Deficient individuals may report depressive symptoms, irritability, or seasonal mood shifts, likely related to vitamin D’s role in neurochemical regulation and inflammation.

- Immune impairment and frequent infections: Vitamin D modulates innate and adaptive immunity. Low levels have been associated with higher rates of respiratory infections and slower wound healing in observational and some interventional studies.

Assessment and confirmation

A serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D test is the standard measure to confirm deficiency. Clinical assessment should consider risk factors such as limited sun exposure, darker skin pigmentation, obesity, malabsorption syndromes, and certain medications that affect vitamin D metabolism.

Management principles

Management is individualized based on baseline levels, symptoms, and comorbidities. Restoring adequate vitamin D typically improves musculoskeletal pain, energy, and immune resilience over weeks to months. In practice, clinicians combine laboratory monitoring, dietary advice, and appropriate supplementation when indicated. Practical considerations

When interpreting symptoms, clinicians rule out alternative causes such as hypothyroidism, anemia, or primary mood disorders. If deficiency is confirmed, daily dosing and rechecking levels after a recommended interval is common practice. Absorption is improved when vitamin D is taken with a meal containing fat.

Conclusion

Vitamin D deficiency can present subtly but has measurable effects on bone health, muscle function, mood, energy, and immunity. Objective testing guides appropriate correction and follow-up. Addressing deficiency—when indicated—can reduce symptom burden and support overall function without replacing evaluation for other potential causes.
