Introduction

Magnesium is an essential mineral involved in hundreds of biochemical reactions, including nerve transmission, muscle function, and energy production. While dietary magnesium is typically safe, supplemental magnesium can produce side effects in some people. The most common adverse reaction is gastrointestinal—particularly diarrhea—although other symptoms such as nausea, abdominal discomfort, muscle weakness, and, rarely, toxicity can occur.

Why diarrhea is most common

Diarrhea is the most frequently reported side effect because several supplemental forms of magnesium act as osmotic laxatives. Unabsorbed magnesium in the intestinal lumen draws water by osmosis, increasing stool liquidity and transit speed. Forms with higher laxative potential include magnesium citrate and magnesium sulfate, while magnesium oxide can produce loose stools because of poor absorption. More gut-friendly forms include magnesium glycinate or malate.

Risk factors and dose considerations

The risk of gastrointestinal effects is dose-dependent and influenced by formulation and individual sensitivity. People with impaired kidney function are less able to excrete excess magnesium and therefore face higher risk of systemic effects. The tolerable upper intake level for supplemental magnesium in adults is generally set at 350 mg/day; this does not include magnesium obtained from food. Starting with a lower dose (for example, 100–200 mg) and titrating upward can reduce the chance of diarrhea and nausea.

Other gastrointestinal complaints

Beyond diarrhea, users may experience nausea, bloating, flatulence, or abdominal cramping—especially when supplements are taken on an empty stomach or in a single large dose. Dividing the daily dose, taking supplements with food, or switching to buffered or slow‑release formulations often mitigates these issues.

Muscle cramps, fatigue, and rare toxicity

Magnesium is commonly used to address muscle cramps, yet paradoxically some people report persistent or new cramps after starting supplements. Causes can include insufficient absorption, imbalances with calcium or potassium, suboptimal magnesium form, or underlying medical conditions. Reports of fatigue or muscle weakness are uncommon but can reflect mineral imbalances or early signs of excess magnesium. Severe magnesium toxicity (hypermagnesemia) is rare in healthy individuals but can cause hypotension, bradycardia, respiratory depression, and, in extreme cases, cardiac arrest—most often in the context of kidney impairment or excessive combined intake of supplements and magnesium‑containing medicines.

Practical guidance

To minimize adverse effects, consider the following evidence‑based steps: begin with a low dose, choose well‑absorbed and gentle forms (e.g., glycinate, malate), take magnesium with food, divide doses through the day, and review concurrent medications. If gastrointestinal symptoms persist or if you have kidney disease or cardiovascular symptoms, seek clinical evaluation.

Summary

Diarrhea is the predominant and most predictable side effect of supplemental magnesium, particularly with osmotic or poorly absorbed forms. Awareness of formulation, dose, and personal health status helps limit adverse effects while allowing potential benefits to be realized.
