Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is an essential, water-soluble nutrient involved in collagen synthesis, immune function, and antioxidant protection. Because the body does not store vitamin C, the question of daily supplementation is common. This article summarizes current evidence-based guidance on whether vitamin C should be taken every day and how to do so safely.

Daily need and dietary sources

Recommended intakes vary by agency, but typical adult values are around 80–90 mg per day. Many people meet this requirement through a diet rich in fruits and vegetables such as citrus, kiwi, strawberries, bell peppers, broccoli, and leafy greens. However, restricted diets, limited access to fresh produce, smoking, certain medical conditions, and high physical stress can increase requirements and make supplementation useful.

Benefits of regular intake

Consistent vitamin C intake supports multiple physiological processes. It contributes to immune cell function and protects cells from oxidative damage by scavenging free radicals. Randomized trials and systematic reviews suggest regular supplementation (often ≥200 mg/day) may modestly shorten the duration and severity of common cold symptoms in the general population, with larger effects observed in people under heavy physical stress. Vitamin C also supports collagen formation, which is important for skin, blood vessels, and wound healing, and it may contribute to cardiovascular health through endothelial support.

Absorption and dosing considerations

Vitamin C is water-soluble and absorbed in the intestine with limited single-dose bioavailability. Evidence indicates absorption efficiency decreases with very large single doses; for that reason, many experts recommend divided doses (for example, 100–200 mg two or three times daily) or formulations designed for sustained release. The European Food Safety Authority and other bodies identify a Tolerable Upper Intake Level of about 2000 mg/day for adults; doses above this can increase the risk of gastrointestinal upset and, in susceptible individuals, kidney stones.

Who may benefit from daily supplementation

Daily supplementation can be reasonable for people with low dietary intake, older adults, smokers (who may require an additional ~35 mg/day), those with certain chronic illnesses, or individuals recovering from surgery or heavy physical exertion. For many others, a balanced diet will provide sufficient vitamin C. Personalized assessment—ideally involving dietary review or laboratory testing when appropriate—helps identify those who would benefit most.

Practical guidance and safety

If choosing to supplement, consider moderate, divided dosing and be mindful of total intake from fortified foods and supplements. Taking vitamin C with meals can reduce stomach discomfort and improves iron absorption when needed. Avoid routinely exceeding the established upper intake level without medical supervision, and discuss supplementation with a healthcare professional if you have kidney disease, a history of kidney stones, or are taking medications that may interact with high-dose vitamin C.
